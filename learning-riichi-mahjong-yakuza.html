Introduction: Riichi Mahjong & Yakuza

The Mahjong minigame in the Yakuza (Like a Dragon) series uses Riichi Mahjong — the modern Japanese variant. It's played with 136 tiles by four players. The game is structurally similar to Rummy or Gin Rummy: you draw and discard tiles, aiming to assemble 4 melds (sets of 3) + 1 pair.

But here's the twist that trips up most newcomers:

⚠️ The Golden Rule

You cannot win simply by completing 4 melds + 1 pair. Your hand must contain at least one Yaku (a qualifying scoring pattern). Dora bonus tiles do NOT count as a Yaku.

This single rule is why your seemingly "complete" hand sometimes won't let you declare a win. Once you internalize the Yaku system, everything clicks.

Step 2: Basic Gameplay & The Goal

2Understand the turn structure and what you're building toward

The Goal: 4 Melds + 1 Pair (+ at least 1 Yaku)

A meld is either:

  • Sequence (Chow / Shuntsu): Three consecutive numbers in the same suit — e.g., 🀈🀉🀊 (3-4-5 of Bamboo)
  • Triplet (Pung / Koutsu): Three identical tiles — e.g., 🀆🀆🀆 (three 6 of Dots)
  • Quad (Kong / Kantsu): Four identical tiles (special case — see Step 5)

🎯 Example Winning Hand

🀊🀋🀌   🀟🀟🀟   🀐🀑🀒   🀖🀗🀘   🀄🀄

(3-4-5 Bam) + (3× 8 Dot) + (1-2-3 Dot) + (7-8-9 Char) + (Pair of East)

Turn Flow

  1. Each player starts with 13 tiles (dealer gets 14)
  2. On your turn: Draw 1 tile → check if you've won → Discard 1 tile
  3. After a discard, other players may call that tile (Pon/Chi/Kan) or declare Ron to win
  4. Play proceeds counterclockwise until someone wins or tiles run out (draw)

Step 4: Building Your Hand Efficiently

4Maximize tile efficiency — the core skill

General Strategy by Game Phase

🌅 Early Game

  • Look for pairs and two-tile sequences in your starting hand
  • Discard isolated Winds & Dragons first (unless you have a pair of them)
  • Then discard isolated terminals (1s & 9s) that have no neighbors
  • Note the Dora indicator and your seat/prevalent wind — they're worth points

☀️ Mid Game

  • Favor two-sided waits: e.g., holding 🀋🀌 (4-5) waits for 3 or 6 — 8 possible tiles
  • Avoid "edge waits" (1-2 needs 3) and "center waits" (4-6 needs 5) when possible
  • Watch discards: if 3 of a tile are already visible, you're waiting on the last copy
  • Don't be afraid to pivot if your planned suit dries up

🌙 Late Game

  • Tap Square (PS) / X (Xbox) every turn to check if Riichi is available
  • Watch what hasn't been discarded — that's likely in someone's hand
  • If ~20 tiles remain and your hand isn't close, try to reach Tenpai (1 away) for draw consolation

🎯 Tile Efficiency Rule of Thumb

Number tiles 3–7 are the most flexible (can form 3 different sequences). Tiles 2 & 8 can form 2 sequences. Tiles 1 & 9 can form only 1 sequence. Honor tiles form zero sequences. This is why you discard honors and terminals first!

"A sequence of adjacent suit tiles (like 3-4 waiting on 2 or 5) has 8 tiles that can complete it. A pair (2-2) only has 2 tiles. Sequences are mathematically easier to complete than triplets."

Step 5: Calling Tiles — Pon, Chi, Kan

5Know when to steal — and usually, don't
CallWhat It DoesFrom WhomConsequence
Pon Take a discard to complete a triplet Any player Hand becomes open — loses Riichi option and some Yaku lose value
ChiTake a discard to complete a sequenceOnly the player to your left
Kan (open)Take a discard to complete a quad; reveals an extra Dora indicatorAny player
Kan (closed)Declare a quad from tiles you drew yourselfYour own handHand stays concealed; still risky (extra Dora helps everyone)

⚠️ The Golden Rule of Stealing

As a beginner: NEVER Pon, Chi, or Kan unless you have a clear plan. Keeping your hand closed gives you access to Riichi (the most reliable Yaku), higher scores, and better defensive options. The only exception is stealing to complete a Dragon triplet or Seat/Prevalent Wind triplet, which gives you a guaranteed Yaku and a fast win.

Step 6: Yaku — The Heart of Riichi Mahjong

6Memorize the common Yaku — this is the key that unlocks winning

A Yaku is a scoring condition your hand must satisfy to be declared a win. No Yaku = No Win. Here are the ones to learn first, ordered by importance:

⭐ The "Sacred 6" Yaku Every Beginner Must Know

💡 The "Men-Tan-Pin" Combo

The most common winning hand pattern in Riichi Mahjong: Menzen Tsumo (1) + Tanyao (1) + Pinfu (1) + Riichi (1) + potential Dora = easily reaches Mangan (5 Han). Aim for this as your default strategy.

Step 7: Going Out — Riichi, Ron & Tsumo

7Declare your win

Riichi Declaration

When your concealed hand is 1 tile away from winning (Tenpai), press Square (PS) / X (Xbox) to declare Riichi. This:

  • Costs 1000 points (a bet — you get it back if you win)
  • Adds 1 Han to your score
  • Locks your hand — you auto-discard everything except your winning tile
  • Unlocks Ura-Dora (underside dora) and chance for Ippatsu
  • Alerts others you're about to win — they may play defensively

🔔 Riichi Pro Tip

Spam the Square (PS) / X (Xbox) button every turn when your hand gets close. The option only appears when you press it, not automatically! The Riichi Helper in the corner shows which tiles you need and how many remain unseen.

Ron vs. Tsumo

Win TypeHowWho Pays
RonWin off an opponent's discard (any player)The discarding player pays everything
TsumoWin off a tile you draw yourselfAll 3 opponents split the cost

Step 8: Dora & Scoring (Han, Fu, Limits)

8Understand how points work

Dora — Bonus Tiles

The face-up tile on the Dead Wall (center of screen) is the Dora indicator. The next tile in sequence is the Dora. Each Dora in your hand = +1 Han. BUT: Dora alone does NOT let you win — you still need a Yaku.

IndicatorDora Tile
3 Bamboo4 Bamboo
9 Dots1 Dots (wraps)
East WindSouth Wind (E→S→W→N→E)
Green DragonRed Dragon (Green→Red→White→Green — alphabetical!)

Red Dora: When enabled, red-numbered 5-tiles count as 1 Dora each. Ura-Dora: If you win after Riichi, tiles under the dora indicators are also revealed — doubling your potential Dora.

Han & Fu → Points

Your score = Han (from Yaku + Dora) × Fu (minipoints from hand structure). The game calculates this automatically.

Step 9: The Furiten Rule

9Avoid the trap that silently blocks your win

Furiten is the rule that causes the most confused frustration for learners. It means: you cannot win by Ron if any of your own discards would complete your hand.

⚠️ Furiten in Plain English

If you are waiting for a 3-Bamboo or 6-Bamboo to win, and you have previously discarded a 3-Bamboo — you are in Furiten. You cannot call Ron on either the 3 or the 6. You can only win by Tsumo (drawing it yourself). Furiten applies to all your waiting tiles, not just the discarded one.

After declaring Riichi, if you miss a winning tile, you are permanently Furiten for the rest of that hand. Never miss a Ron call after Riichi!

Step 10: Defensive Play & Reading Discards

10Stop feeding wins to the AI

🛡️ When to Fold

  • If an opponent declares Riichi and your hand is far from ready, fold immediately
  • Discard tiles the Riichi player has already discarded (they're Furiten on these)
  • Also safe: tiles discarded after they declared Riichi (by you or others)
  • Breaking up a Pung gives you 3 safe discards in a row

🔍 Reading AI Patterns

  • If a suit is absent from their discards, they may be building a Flush — avoid that suit
  • If they discard many honors/terminals early, they're likely going for Tanyao (All Simples)
  • The 1-4-7, 2-5-8, 3-6-9 rule: If someone discards a 4, the 1 and 7 of the same suit are statistically safer (because they'd need a paired wait)

Equipment You'll Need

🀄 The Tile Set

  • Japanese-size tiles (smaller than Chinese) — 26mm tall standard
  • 136 tiles: 108 suited + 28 honors. No flowers, no jokers.
  • Optional: Red 5 tiles (Aka Dora) — one red 5 per suit
  • Budget starter set: $30–50 on Amazon (plastic, functional)
  • Quality set: $100–300 (heavier resin, better feel)
  • Premium: $500+ (bone & bamboo, hand-carved)

🧮 Point Sticks (Tenbou)

  • 10,000-point sticks — ornate, largest
  • 5,000-point sticks — medium, often with dots
  • 1,000-point sticks — smaller (used for Riichi bet)
  • 100-point sticks — smallest (used for Honba counters)
  • Each player starts with 25,000 points (varies by ruleset)
  • Common starter distribution: 1×10k, 2×5k, 4×1k, 10×100

🛒 Additional Gear

  • Mahjong mat (Jang Mat): A padded cloth surface (~80cm square) that dampens tile noise and provides a smooth playing surface. Essential for home play. ~$20–40.
  • Dice: Two standard dice included with most sets.
  • Compass marker / wind indicator: A small tray or marker showing East/South/West/North to track round wind and seat positions.
  • Scoring chart / Cheat sheet: Absolutely acceptable for beginners. Many clubs provide laminated reference cards.
  • Yaku reference sheet: Keep one handy until you've memorized them.

Basic IRL Table Etiquette — The "Don't Look Like a Yakuza-Only Player" Guide

Etiquette in riichi mahjong serves two purposes: preventing cheating and keeping the game flowing. These rules come from Japanese jansou (mahjong parlors) and are observed at all levels from casual clubs to the World Riichi Championship.

🚨 The Absolute Essentials (Do These or Look Rude)

RuleWhy It Matters
Draw and discard with ONE HAND onlyUsing two hands is associated with tile-switching tricks. The only exceptions: sorting your starting tiles, straightening your hand, revealing tiles on a win, and pushing the wall forward.
Keep discards in neat rows of 6 tilesDisorganized discards make it impossible to track what was discarded when — critical for Furiten and defensive reading.
Call out loud: "Pon!", "Chi!", "Kan!", "Riichi!", "Ron!", "Tsumo!"Must be audible to all players. Call before you touch any tiles. Grabbing a tile then calling is a dead hand (penalty).
For Chi: wait a beat before revealing your tilesSomeone might call Pon, which takes priority. Protecting your hand info is good practice — though late "interruption Pon" is considered inelegant.
Place called sets to your RIGHT, not in front of youKeeps the center clear. Rotate the called tile sideways to show who discarded it: left tile = player to your left, middle = across, right = player to right.

When you win by Ron, do NOT take the tileLeave the winning tile in the discarder's pond. Only on TV broadcasts is the tile moved to the winner's hand (and that's solely for the camera).

Etiquette by Level of Formality

🟢 Friendly / Casual

  • Reference sheets and Yaku lists are welcome
  • Asking "what should I discard?" is fine
  • Friendly table talk, occasional jokes
  • Mistakes are laughed off, not penalized
  • Perfect for: home games, teaching sessions, bar meetups
  • Don't: use your phone excessively, comment on your own hand ("wow my hand is terrible"), imitate anime tropes

🟡 Club / Semi-Formal

  • No reference materials on the table
  • Minimal table talk; focus on the game
  • Quick pace expected — don't stall
  • No commenting on other people's plays
  • Keep hands concealed after the hand ends
  • Don't check the dead wall or next draws after a hand concludes
  • Perfect for: club nights, interclub matches

🔴 Tournament / Competitive

  • No talking except calls and score announcements
  • No phones, smartwatches, or earpieces
  • No reaction to draws (no sighs, no smiles)
  • No eating at the table
  • Use minimal point sticks for payments (e.g., one 5k stick, not five 1k sticks)
  • Score is announced: base score first, then total with Honba
  • Don't thank players for payment or apologize for winning
  • Perfect for: EMA tournaments, WRC events, ranked competitions

Common IRL Mistakes Yakuza Players Make

❌ Calling Tiles Too Slowly

In Yakuza, the game pauses and shows you a prompt. IRL, if you hesitate for even a second after someone discards, the next player will draw — and your chance to call that tile is gone forever. Stay alert during every discard, not just your own turn.

❌ Not Keeping the Drawn Tile Separate

In Yakuza, you can sort tiles freely. IRL, many clubs expect you to place the drawn tile to the side of your hand, discard, and only then incorporate kept tiles. This prevents confusion about what was just drawn vs. what was already in your hand — critical for Furiten tracking.

❌ Forgetting to Track Furiten

Yakuza warns you with a Furiten icon. IRL, nothing warns you. You must remember every tile you've discarded. Calling Ron while in Furiten is a Chombo (penalty) — typically a Mangan payment to all players and a re-deal. This is the #1 penalty committed by digital-to-IRL converts.

❌ Playing Too Slowly

Yakuza lets you ponder each discard indefinitely. IRL, there are no time banks. At tournaments, shot clocks as short as 5 seconds + 10 seconds reserve are common. At casual tables, excessively slow play is seen as disrespectful to everyone's time.

❌ Incorrect Point Payments

Use the minimum number of sticks. Paying 1,000 points with ten 100-point sticks because "you have too many" is considered rude — it slows the game and leaves you with the wrong denominations. Exchange sticks between hands if needed.

❌ Not Knowing Your Score

In Yakuza, the game tells you the score. IRL, the winner is expected to announce their own score. For Tsumo wins by non-dealer: announce the non-dealer payment first, then the dealer payment. Example: "1,000 - 2,000" (meaning non-dealers pay 1,000 each, dealer pays 2,000).

IRL Wall Setup — The Quick Version

This is what Yakuza does for you:

  1. Shuffle: All tiles face-down. Scramble them thoroughly. Don't be violent — gentle shoving motion.
  2. Build walls: Each player builds a row of 17 tiles (face-down), then stacks a second row of 17 on top. Total: 34 tiles per wall, two tiles high.
  3. Form the square: Push all four walls together into a closed square.
  4. Roll dice (dealer): Dealer rolls two dice. Count counterclockwise starting from yourself. The result points at the wall to break.
  5. Break the wall: The player whose wall was chosen counts from the right end of their wall, equal to the dice roll, and separates the wall there. Tiles to the left = draw wall. The 14 tiles to the right = dead wall.
  6. Flip Dora: The third tile from the right end of the dead wall (top row) is flipped as the Dora indicator.
  7. Deal tiles: Starting from the break, deal 4 tiles at a time (two stacks), counterclockwise, until all have 12 tiles. Then deal 1 more tile each (13). Dealer takes a 14th tile as their first draw.

👋 What to Expect at Your First IRL Session

Most clubs are extremely welcoming to beginners, especially if you already know the rules from Yakuza. Let them know it's your first time playing with real tiles — they'll help with wall building, scoring, and pacing. Bring a Yaku reference sheet and a scoring chart. Expect to play much slower than Yakuza — 8–12 hands per hour vs. 20+. Expect to make etiquette mistakes; nobody will be harsh. The community is small and eager to grow. Within 3–4 sessions, most Yakuza players feel completely comfortable at a real table.

🆕 Step 14: Playing Online — Against Real Humans

14Graduate from Yakuza AI to the global riichi community

Yakuza's AI opponents are predictable. They follow patterns. They don't bluff. They don't adapt. Playing against real humans online is the single best way to improve — but you need to understand what you're getting into. The skills that crush Yakuza AI will get you destroyed by competent human players if you don't adjust.

Platform Deep Dives

🌟 Mahjong Soul (MajSoul / 雀魂) — Your Best First Step

Why start here: Native English support, polished tutorial, familiar anime aesthetic, and enough assists to bridge the gap from Yakuza's automated helpers. The Bronze room is region-locked, meaning you'll only face other beginners in your region — a gentle on-ramp.

Assists available: Wait preview (shows which tiles complete your hand), Furiten warning, Dora highlighting, shanten counter. These are disabled in the highest rooms (Throne room), so you'll naturally wean off them as you climb.

Time controls: 5 seconds + 20 seconds reserve per turn. Comfortable pace for learners.

Rooms: Bronze → Silver → Gold → Jade → Throne. Hanchan (East+South) and Tonpuusen (East only) available.

Gacha warning: Characters and cosmetics are obtained via gacha. Purely cosmetic — zero gameplay impact. But it can be expensive if you get hooked. One free character has been earnable ever, and she's unobtainable now. Set a budget or ignore cosmetics entirely.

Community: Large English-speaking Discord communities. Active subreddit (r/mahjongsoul). Streamers and content creators.

🔥 Tenhou.net — The Deep End (Read Before Playing)

Tenhou is Japan's most competitive online mahjong server, and the standard against which serious players measure themselves. But it has a very specific ruleset that can warp your play if you don't understand its design.

⚠️ The Tenhou Trap: 4th-Place Avoidance Mentality

Tenhou's ranking system was specifically designed to punish 4th place extremely harshly. The gap between 3rd and 4th is roughly 4× larger than the gap between 2nd and 3rd. This was deliberately engineered to prevent players from disconnecting when behind — but it creates a specific, defensive playstyle:

  • Tenhou players learn to avoid coin flips and risky gambles
  • They prioritize not dealing in over winning big hands
  • They fold more conservatively than in any other ruleset

Professional players warn: "Stop playing like this is Tenhou." The defensive habits that rank you up online can make you too passive in tournaments, clubs, and other online platforms where 1st place is rewarded proportionally. A 50-50 1st-or-4th gamble is mathematically good in most rulesets — but terrible on Tenhou.

💡 How to Use Tenhou Correctly

  • Use Tenhou as output, not input — play there to measure your skill after studying theory
  • Supplement Tenhou with play on Mahjong Soul or at local clubs to maintain a balanced style
  • Understand why you're making different decisions on different platforms
  • Don't spam games mindlessly; review your replays and learn from your mistakes
  • The road to 6-dan is achievable with disciplined, conservative play. Real mahjong starts after 6-dan.

Mindset Shifts for Human Play

✅ Do This

  • Learn Dama (silent Tenpai): If your hand is already worth 3+ Han without Riichi, consider staying silent. You'll catch more Ron calls and can still fold if danger appears.
  • Track discards actively: Watch what suits opponents aren't discarding. Missing suit = Flush danger.
  • Fold early, fold often: Against human Riichi, if your hand is 2-shanten or worse, folding is almost always correct.
  • Review your games: Most platforms save replays. Watch your losses — what could you have done differently?
  • Use the community: Post hand reviews on Reddit or Discord. Getting a stronger player to review your decisions is the fastest way to improve.

❌ Don't Do This

  • Don't Riichi every Tenpai: Yakuza AI is bad enough that Riichi → win works reliably. Humans fold to Riichi. Sometimes Dama is better.
  • Don't ignore defense: In Yakuza, dealing in is annoying. Online, dealing into a Haneman can lose you the entire match.
  • Don't autopilot: Playing on autopilot while watching TV might work against Yakuza AI. It will get you crushed by humans.
  • Don't tilt: Bad beats happen. A string of 4th places happens. Tilt leads to more mistakes. Take breaks.
  • Don't assume everyone plays like Yakuza AI: They don't. Humans are more defensive, more deceptive, and more unpredictable.

Quick-Reference: Online Etiquette

  • Queue when you have time: Don't start a hanchan (East+South round, ~30–45 min) if you might need to leave. Disconnecting mid-game ruins it for everyone.
  • Don't stall: Intentionally running down the timer when losing is considered toxic. Most platforms have auto-kick for repeated slow play.
  • No collusion: Don't coordinate with friends in ranked queues. This is cheating and detectable.
  • Emotes are fine, spam is not: A well-timed emote after a big hand is fun. Spamming emotes every 2 seconds tilts people.
  • GG at the end: A simple "GG" or thumbs-up emote after the match is good sportsmanship, regardless of where you placed.

🔄 How Online Play Improves Your Yakuza Game

Playing against humans online will make you a dramatically better Yakuza player:

  • You'll recognize Yaku faster: High-volume play builds pattern recognition that makes Yakuza's AI feel slow and predictable.
  • Your defense will improve: Learning to fold against human Riichi translates directly to avoiding deal-ins against AI.
  • You'll understand tile efficiency: Human opponents punish inefficient discards. You'll quickly learn to optimize.
  • Scoring will become intuitive: After hundreds of human games, Mangan/Haneman requirements become second nature — making Completion List goals much easier.
  • Furiten awareness will be automatic: Playing without the safety net of a Furiten warning trains you to track discards subconsciously.

Many Yakuza completionists report that after 100+ games on Mahjong Soul, returning to Yakuza's Mahjong tables feels like playing on easy mode.

Common Mistakes & FAQ

❌ "I have 4 melds + a pair, why can't I win?"

You're missing a Yaku. Dora does not count. You must have at least one scoring pattern from the Yaku table above. Declare Riichi with a closed hand for an automatic 1 Han.

❌ "Why can't I call Ron on this tile I need?"

You're likely in Furiten: one of your own discards would also complete your hand. Check your discard pile. You can only win by Tsumo. Also, if you're going for Tanyao and the winning tile is a 1 or 9 — that breaks the Yaku, so the game will not offer Ron.

❌ "Why can't I declare Riichi?"

Requirements: (1) Hand must be fully concealed (no Pon/Chi/Kan steals), (2) you must be Tenpai (1 tile away), (3) at least 4 tiles remain in the wall, (4) you have 1000 points to stake. Remember to press Square/X — the option won't appear on its own!

❓ "Should I ever Pon or Chi?"

Yes, but rarely: (1) To complete a Dragon/Seat/Prevailing Wind triplet (gives a guaranteed Yaku), (2) To rush a cheap win as dealer to retain dealership, (3) When you already have 2+ Dora and just need to close, (4) Late in the hand to reach Tenpai for draw consolation (split of 3000 points).

❓ "What difficulty table should I play on?"

For learning: Easy table. For money/Completion: Hard table (higher ¥ per point). The AI difficulty difference is relatively small — luck is the bigger factor. For Completion List Mangan/Haneman, play on Easy where you'll have more time to shape your hand.

❓ "How does the Dealer bonus work?"

When you're East (Dealer), your wins are worth ~50% more points. BUT, if someone else wins by Ron off your discard, you pay extra too. If the Dealer wins, they stay as dealer for an extra hand (Renchan), racking up Honba bonuses (+300 per consecutive dealer win/draw).

💡 "Is there any way to cheat in Yakuza Mahjong?"

Some games have cheat items: Yakuza Kiwami has the Lucky Tile (Locker I2) for auto-Thirteen Orphans. Yakuza: Dead Souls has a Lucky Mahjong Marker from Erika's hostess quest. Yakuza 0, Kiwami 2, and most others have no cheat items — you must learn the game.

🆕 "I'm good at Yakuza Mahjong — will I get destroyed online?"

Initially, yes — and that's okay. Yakuza AI doesn't play defense properly, never uses Dama effectively, and doesn't adapt to you. Expect to lose a lot in your first ~20 online games. Focus on learning, not winning. After 50–100 online games, you'll likely be stronger than 90% of Yakuza-only players. Then return to Yakuza and marvel at how easy it feels.

🆕 "What's the minimum etiquette for my first IRL club visit?"

Six things: (1) Draw/discard with one hand, (2) keep discards in rows of 6, (3) call Pon/Chi/Kan/Ron/Tsumo out loud, (4) don't grab tiles before calling, (5) be ready for your turn — keep up the pace, (6) tell them it's your first time with physical tiles. That covers 90% of first-timer etiquette mistakes.
