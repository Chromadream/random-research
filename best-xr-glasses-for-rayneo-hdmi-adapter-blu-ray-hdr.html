Best XR Glasses to Pair with a RayNeo HDMI 2.0 Adapter & a Blu-ray Player

Use case: watching Blu-ray movies only — content is Full HD (1080p) or 4K with HDR. 4K output is not required, but HDR support is prioritized. A non-HDR option is acceptable if it is clearly superior.

Bottom line: Buy the RayNeo Air 4 Pro

The RayNeo Air 4 Pro (~$299, often $249–254 on sale) is the clear winner. It is literally the glasses RayNeo designed to pair with your HDMI 2.0 adapter — RayNeo sells the two as a "$339 Air 4 Pro + HDMI Adapter" bundle — and it was the world's first AR glasses with true HDR10 support, which is exactly your top priority.

Strong runner-up with HDR: X by Xreal (XBX) a01+ ($299) — brighter (1,600 nits) and lighter (62 g), also native HDR10. Best "superior non-HDR" option if you are willing to drop HDR: Xreal One (~$399–499) for its spatial anchoring, electrochromic dimming, and Bose audio.

1. First, a critical check on your adapter version

RayNeo sells two different HDMI adapters, and this matters enormously for HDR:

  • HDR10 version (Amazon ASIN B0GHY8M6KG, ~$59–64): "Upgraded Version with HDR10 Support," 4K@30Hz, 2K@30Hz, 1080p@120Hz, built-in battery. This is the one explicitly made for the Air 4 Pro.
  • Non-HDR version (ASIN B0FFSCSZ8V): "…for Air 3/Air 2 Series (Not Support HDR10)."
If you own the non-HDR version, no HDR signal will reach any glasses. You would need to upgrade to the HDR10 adapter ($59–64) to get HDR from your Blu-ray player at all. If your adapter is labeled simply "HDMI 2.0 to Type-C" without an HDR10 badge, check the model — the HDR-capable one is the newer SKU marketed for the Air 4 Pro.

Also relevant: RayNeo confirms its glasses, Pocket TV, and the HDMI-to-USB-C adapter support the HDCP protocol, which is required for copy-protected Blu-ray discs to play. That is a big deal for your use case (many cheap third-party adapters explicitly tell you to "disable HDCP," which would black-screen protected discs).

Why it wins for your use case

  • Guaranteed, vendor-tested match with the RayNeo HDMI 2.0 (HDR10) adapter — the exact pairing RayNeo bundles.
  • True native HDR10 — the feature you're prioritizing — with 10-bit color and 1,200 nits; reviewers call the picture "dirt cheap HDR greatness" (Tom's Guide).
  • Movie Mode color preset and B&O audio make it the most "home-theater-like" of the cheap glasses.

  • Cheap-feeling plastic and a soft case.

7. Blu-ray-specific reality check (things you should know)

HDR10 vs. Dolby Vision

The RayNeo adapter and both HDR glasses support HDR10 only — there is no Dolby Vision anywhere in this category of glasses. Good news: virtually all Dolby Vision Blu-ray discs include a mandatory HDR10 base layer, so they will still play in HDR10. For 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray discs, set your player's HDR output to HDR10 (disable DV) for the most predictable result.

4K will be downscaled to 1080p

There are no consumer 4K micro-OLED AR glasses yet — every candidate above is 1080p per eye. A 4K HDR Blu-ray will be downscaled to 1080p, but the HDR10 dynamic-range metadata passes through (the adapter advertises "4K HDR transmission" with HDCP), so you keep the wide color/high contrast even at 1080p. You're not losing HDR; you're only losing spatial resolution.

3D Blu-ray (frame-packed) will not work

These glasses and adapters support side-by-side (SBS) 3D — not the frame-packed 3D format used by 3D Blu-ray discs. Do not expect 3D Blu-ray discs to play in 3D through this chain. (The glasses' 3D modes are for SBS files/streams and AI 2D→3D conversion, not frame-packed HDMI 3D.)

24p and judder

The adapter advertises 4K@30Hz and 1080p@60/120Hz — there is no native 24Hz mode. Your player will output 24fps film content as 60Hz (3:2 pulldown), so you may see subtle motion judder on slow pans. This is common to all AR glasses via HDMI adapters, not specific to RayNeo. If it bothers you, keep the glasses at 60Hz and let the player do its normal 24→60 conversion.

Audio

The glasses' speakers are fed over the DisplayPort audio channel. Set the Blu-ray player's audio output to PCM / stereo downmix so the adapter passes two-channel audio the glasses can play. Bitstreamed Dolby TrueHD / DTS-HD MA may not be decoded by the adapter/glasses chain.

Copy protection (HDCP)

RayNeo explicitly states its official accessories (including the HDMI adapter) support HDCP for licensed content — essential for Blu-ray. Using a no-name HDMI→USB-C adapter instead can produce a black screen on protected discs (several third-party listings literally say "disable HDCP in the source"). Stick with the official RayNeo adapter you already have.

8. Recommended setup checklist

  • Confirm your RayNeo adapter is the HDR10 version (ASIN B0GHY8M6KG), not the Air 3/Air 2 "Not Support HDR10" version.
  • Blu-ray player → HDMI cable → RayNeo HDMI 2.0 adapter → (USB-C) → RayNeo Air 4 Pro.
  • Charge the adapter's built-in battery first (it won't power the glasses reliably if dead).
  • On the glasses, select HDR10 display mode (via the hardware menu) and Movie color mode.
  • On the player: HDR output = HDR10; audio = PCM stereo; deep color/24p settings can stay on (24p will be converted by the adapter).
  • If HDR10 flickers on a given disc, fall back to the glasses' "AI HDR" mode (SDR→HDR upscaling) — it still looks punchy.
  • For real 3D movies, use SBS content (files/apps), not frame-packed 3D Blu-ray discs.
