🧠 The Strategy: One Handheld, One Mission

The key insight: all four share the exact same 3.5″ 960×640 3:2 display. What differentiates them is form factor, controls, chipset, battery, build quality, and design language. The strategy below assigns each device a primary identity that plays to its unique strengths, with secondary roles that fill gaps naturally.

🎖️ 1. Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic — The Premium GBA Showcase

Primary Role: The definitive GBA experience

This is the handheld you hand to a friend and say "look at what GBA games can look like." Its CNC aluminum body, borderless glass front, and Famicom-style colorways (especially Retro Gold) make it the most photogenic device in your fleet. The Helio G99 is overkill for GBA, which is exactly the point — it runs 4× integer scaling flawlessly and has headroom for:

  • Light-to-medium CRT shaders (lottes, easymode, fakelottes, zfast, scanline overlays). The G99 can't do CRT-Royale, but it handles a surprising amount of shader work that budget Linux handhelds choke on.
  • Fast-forward through grinding in Pokémon ROM hacks or Golden Sun without breaking a sweat.
  • Android-native pixel-art games like TMNT: Shredder's Revenge, Dead Cells, Stardew Valley — controller-compatible titles that feel at home on a no-stick layout.

GBA Use Case: Showcase & Pick-Up-and-Play

ScenarioVerdict
🎥 Showcase / video piece★ BEST The metal body, glass front, and Retro Gold colorway photograph beautifully. No sticks = cleaner aesthetic. The 4× integer-scaled GBA image on this panel is genuinely stunning on camera.
⚡ Pick-up-and-play★ Great No sticks means no accidental inputs. Android sleep/wake is instant. The candy-bar shape slips into a pocket easily. The 2,600 mAh battery (~5–6 hours) is sufficient for commute sessions but not marathon days.
📖 Longer RPGs⚠ Decent but limited The sharp rectangular edges can cause hand fatigue over 2+ hour sessions. The battery is the smallest in the fleet. Metal body feels great but adds weight (227g). Use for RPGs in shorter bursts.

Secondary Roles

  • SNES / Genesis with shaders — the 3:2 screen handles 4:3 content with thin black sidebars that blend into the bezel. Combined with a light CRT shader, this is a sublime 16-bit experience.
  • PS1 (2× upscale + shader) — the G99 handles this easily. No analog stick needed for most PS1 RPGs (FFVII–IX, Chrono Cross, Suikoden).
  • Pico-8 — the G99 runs Pico-8 natively via the Android app, and the small sharp screen is perfect for it.
⚠ Avoid: N64 (no stick), PSP (black bars + no stick), GameCube/PS2 (practically unplayable without analog). Don't force it into roles it wasn't designed for.

⚡ 2. Ayaneo Pocket Micro 2 — The Pocket Powerhouse

Primary Role: The most capable 3:2 micro-device that exists

The Snapdragon 865 + Adreno 650 with mature Turnip driver support is the entire point of this device. It represents a ~220% performance uplift over the G99 in the Classic, opening up entire console libraries that the other three can't touch. The recessed TMR sticks, larger face buttons, 3,950 mAh battery (~52% larger than Classic), and 3.5mm headphone jack make it the most versatile device in your collection by a wide margin.

GBA Use Case: Longer RPGs

ScenarioVerdict
📖 Longer RPGs★ BEST The slightly larger body (162×67.8mm vs 156×63mm on Classic), bigger battery (3,950 vs 2,600 mAh), and recessed sticks that don't get in the way make this the most comfortable device for extended sessions. 5–8 hours on 2D titles. Fast-forward through grinding at full speed.
🎥 Showcase★ Great Same gorgeous metal build as the Classic. The Stardust Purple variant in particular is a stunner. The sticks give it a more "modern premium" look vs the Classic's "pure retro" vibe — different aesthetic, equally photogenic.
⚡ Pick-up-and-play★ Great Android instant wake, excellent sleep battery drain. Slightly heavier (248g) and the recessed sticks mean it's not quite as sleek as the Classic for pure D-pad games, but still very pocketable.

Primary Mission: 5th & 6th Gen Console Emulation

This is where the Micro 2 earns its keep. Systems the other three devices cannot handle well:

  • N64 — Full speed, 2× upscale. TMR sticks solve the Classic's biggest limitation. GoldenEye, Perfect Dark, Mario 64, Ocarina of Time, Majora's Mask — all playable with proper analog control.
  • Dreamcast — Near-flawless across the entire library. SoulCalibur, Shenmue, Jet Set Radio, Crazy Taxi with analog stick + triggers.
  • PSP — Most library at 2× resolution. The 3:2 screen means black bars (~20% of screen), but on a 3.5″ panel the image is still very usable. God of War, Persona 3 Portable, Crisis Core.
  • Saturn — Historically difficult to emulate, the SD865 with Vulkan handles it well. Panzer Dragoon, NiGHTS, Guardian Heroes.
  • GameCube — Most library runs well. Wind Waker, Metroid Prime, Paper Mario TTYD, Mario Sunshine, Luigi's Mansion. The Adreno 650's mature Vulkan drivers make this possible.
  • PS2 (selective) — Cherry-pick lighter titles. Final Fantasy X, Kingdom Hearts, God of War (with tweaks), Persona 4. Not a full-PS2 replacement, but impressive for this size.

Secret Weapon: Android Gaming & Streaming

Wi-Fi 6 + SD865 = excellent cloud streaming. Xbox Cloud Gaming, Steam Link, Moonlight all run smoothly. The 3:2 screen letterboxes 16:9 streams with manageable bars. Also handles native Android ports (GTA: San Andreas, Hollow Knight port, Vampire Survivors) far better than the other three.

⚠ Avoid: Switch emulation (out of scope for SD865), heavy PS2 titles (Shadow of the Colossus, Gran Turismo 4), Wii games needing motion controls. The 3.5″ screen is genuinely small for 6th-gen UIs — be realistic.

🍑 3. KONKR Pocket Advance (Peach) — The Nostalgia Charm Piece

Primary Role: The "Hello Kitty GBA" — a collector's joy

Let's be honest: you bought the Peach edition because it's adorable and it evokes the pink/hello-kitty-adjacent GBA modding culture. This is not your powerhouse. This is not your daily driver. This is the handheld that makes you smile when you see it on the shelf, and that's a valid use case in a collection. It's a conversation starter, a display piece, and a perfectly competent budget GBA machine wrapped in an irresistible shell.

GBA Use Case: The Feel-Good Pick-Up-and-Play & Display Piece

ScenarioVerdict
🎥 Showcase / shelf piece★ BEST (for its niche) The Peach colorway is unique in the market. It photographs wonderfully against pastel backgrounds. The GBA-inspired silhouette is instantly recognizable. It's the handheld friends will pick up first out of curiosity. It tells a story: "this is what a modern Hello Kitty GBA could have been."
⚡ Pick-up-and-play★ Great The curved GBA-style body is arguably the most ergonomic shape of all four devices — it was literally designed for human hands by Nintendo's industrial designers. 224g is well balanced. The 4,000 mAh battery is the second-largest. No sticks means pure D-pad simplicity.
📖 Longer RPGs★ Great The curved body + lightweight plastic build + 4,000 mAh battery = surprisingly good for long sessions. The G90T handles GBA/SNES/PS1 with ease. Active cooling keeps it comfortable. This could genuinely be your "curl up on the couch and play Golden Sun for 3 hours" device.

Secondary Roles

  • Light PS1 RPGs — The G90T handles PS1 with room to spare. Final Fantasy Tactics, Suikoden II, Breath of Fire IV. The 4,000 mAh battery means long sessions.
  • SNES / Genesis — The curved GBA form factor is genuinely comfortable for D-pad-centric 16-bit action.
  • ROM hack exploration — With a microSD card loaded with Pokémon ROM hacks, this becomes a dedicated "discovery" device. The budget price means you worry less about tossing it in a bag.
⚠ Note: The Helio G90T sits between the G85 (One35) and G99 (Classic). It handles Dreamcast and PSP but struggles with GameCube/PS2. The 4GB RAM in your Peach model is adequate for retro gaming but limits multitasking. Don't expect the Micro 2's emulation ceiling. This is not a flaw — it's a feature. Embrace the constraint.

🔄 4. MagicX One35 — The TATE Mode Specialist

Primary Role: Vertical arcade gaming & dual-orientation versatility

This is the most unique device in your collection and the one that fills a gap none of the others can. The dual-D-pad layout, dedicated TATE shoulder buttons, and Dawn Launcher's per-game orientation switching make this the definitive vertical-gaming handheld on a 3:2 panel. No other device in your fleet can do what this does.

GBA Use Case: Secondary

ScenarioVerdict
📖 GBA in general⚠ Functional but outclassed The One35 does GBA perfectly well — same 4× integer scale, same 3:2 panel. But the rattly plastic build, awkward shoulder buttons, and budget feel mean the other three devices all offer a better GBA experience. Use this for GBA only when the others aren't handy, or for GBA titles that benefit from the unique controls.

Primary Mission: TATE / Vertical Gaming

Rotate the device 90° clockwise and a second set of controls activates. This unlocks:

  • Vertical arcade shmups (TATE mode) — This is the One35's raison d'être. DoDonPachi, Espgaluda, Ikaruga (Dreamcast), 1942/1943 (Capcom), Raiden, Gunbird, Strikers 1945, Battle Garegga, Batsugun, Mushihimesama. The 3:2 screen in vertical orientation gives you a roughly 3:2→2:3 aspect ratio, which is fantastic for vertical shooters. The Dawn Launcher makes orientation switching a first-class feature per game.
  • Nintendo DS (vertical stacked screens) — DraStic comes pre-configured for vertical orientation. The two screens stack perfectly. Pokémon Black/White, Phantom Hourglass, Castlevania: Dawn of Sorrow, Mario Kart DS. The 3.5″ screen is small for dual-screen DS, but it works surprisingly well.
  • WonderSwan / WonderSwan Color — Native vertical-orientation handheld. The 3:2 panel maps beautifully. Gunpei Yokoi's final hardware design lives on.
  • MAME vertical arcade games — Pac-Man, Donkey Kong, Galaga, Dig Dug — all play in their native vertical orientation.

Secondary Roles

  • N64 lite — The single Hall-effect analog stick is excellent quality. Mario 64, Paper Mario, Ocarina of Time. The G85 handles N64 well, and the One35 has the best analog stick among the non-Micro-2 devices (the only one with an analog stick, in fact, other than Micro 2).
  • Android budget gaming — The Dawn Launcher includes touch-to-button mapping. Older Android titles (Jetpack Joyride, early Asphalt, Shattered Pixel Dungeon) run fine on the G85.
  • The "beater" handheld — At $55–$95 with a plastic shell and 4,300 mAh battery, this is the device you toss in a bag without a case. It's also the lightest at 189g.
⚠ Known issues: The TATE shoulder buttons require a "claw grip" that can be awkward for long sessions. The A/B/X/Y buttons can rattle. The microSD slot orientation is tricky (inserting upside-down can cause the card to fall inside). Minimum brightness is still quite bright. Treat these as quirks, not dealbreakers — the TATE functionality is genuinely unique at this price.

Since GBA is the common thread across all four devices, here's a focused comparison for the three GBA scenarios you asked about:

🥈 Pocket Advance (Peach)
Ergonomic curved body + 4,000 mAh = close second. Slightly weaker chip means fast-forward isn't as snappy.

🥉 Micro Classic

🥈 Pocket Advance (Peach)
The Hello Kitty GBA tribute is a conversation piece. Different kind of "wow" — nostalgic, cute, accessible. Photographs wonderfully for social content.

🥉 Micro 2 (Stardust Purple)
Same premium build as Classic. The GameCube-inspired purple + RGB stick rings = modern premium wow. Different vibe: "look what this tiny thing can run."

💡 Creative "Beyond the Obvious" Ideas

📸 Content Creation Angle

You own four visually distinct handhelds with the same screen. This is comparison content gold:

  • Same game, four devices: Load the same GBA game on all four and create a video/show friends the difference in how the same image feels on different hardware. The screen is identical — the experience is not. This illustrates why build quality, ergonomics, and design matter.
  • "The $89 GBA vs the $239 GBA": Pocket Advance (Peach) vs Micro Classic running identical GBA content. Is the premium worth 2.7× the price? This type of content performs extremely well.
  • TATE mode showcase: The One35 running DoDonPachi in TATE mode is visually striking and unlike anything most people have seen on a handheld.

🎯 Themed Collections on Each Device

  • Micro Classic: "The Greatest Hits" — the top 20 GBA games of all time, curated and beautifully presented through ES-DE with custom art. This is your museum device.
  • Micro 2: "Beyond GBA" — GC, PS2, N64, Dreamcast. The device that says "yes, this tiny thing can run that."
  • Pocket Advance (Peach): "Cozy & Cute" — Harvest Moon: Friends of Mineral Town, Kirby & the Amazing Mirror, Hamtaro: Ham-Ham Heartbreak, Pokémon (any), WarioWare, Rhythm Tengoku (translated). Games that match the vibe of the pink shell.
  • One35: "Vertical Virtuosos" — Every great vertical shmup, TATE-mode arcade game, and DS title you can fit. This is your dedicated "rotate and play" library.

The Helio G99, G90T, and G85 all share the limitation of not being able to run heavy shaders like CRT-Royale. Here's what does work well on each tier:

💡 Recommendation: On GBA, skip CRT shaders entirely and use an LCD grid shader (LCD3x) — it mimics the original unlit GBA screen's pixel grid, which is more authentic to the handheld experience and far cheaper on the GPU. Save CRT shaders for SNES/Genesis on the Micro Classic or Micro 2.
