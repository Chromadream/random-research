🎮 The 3:2 Quartet: A Strategy Guide

Maximizing four retro handhelds with identical 3.5″ 960×640 3:2 displays — no overlap wasted, every strength exploited.

📐 The Common Thread

All four devices share the same 3.5″ IPS panel at 960×640 (3:2, ~330 PPI). This is the perfect integer-scaled canvas for Game Boy Advance (4× point-to-point at 960×640 fills the screen exactly). But each handheld brings wildly different chipsets, controls, and philosophies — and that's where the strategy unfolds.

🥇 Device #1: Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic

🎯 Role: The GBA Shrine — Premium 4× Shader Showcase + 8/16-bit CRT Canvas

Why This Role?

The Pocket Micro Classic is the only metal-bodied device in the quartet. It feels premium. Pair that with the Helio G99 — a chip that is hilariously overpowered for GBA — and you've got a device whose very excess becomes its strength: heavy CRT & LCD shader pipelines that would choke weaker chips run flawlessly here.

🎨 Creative Assignment: The "Reference" GBA + 16-bit Console Experience

  1. Primary Library: Game Boy Advance — This is the core. 4× integer scale (240×4 = 960, 160×4 = 640) fills the 3:2 screen perfectly with zero interpolation blur. Combine with the mGBA core in RetroArch and layer on:
    • CRT-Royale or CRT-Lottes shader for that "playing on a tiny CRT TV" vibe
    • LCD3x or Grid shader for authentic GBA pixel-grid reproduction (simulates the original unlit GBA screen)
    • Color correction (GBA desaturation → vivid) to mimic the original GBA's undersaturated panel
  2. Secondary Library: 16-bit Consoles (SNES / Genesis / NES / TG-16 / PC Engine) — These are 4:3 or 8:7 systems that display beautifully on the 3:2 panel with small horizontal bars. The G99's extra power means:
    • Run BSNES-HD or Mesen cores with heavy CRT shaders at full speed
    • Enable run-ahead latency reduction (1–2 frames) for virtually zero input lag on SNES/Genesis
    • Integer-scale with interlacing shaders for that authentic 240p CRT look
  3. N64 via D-pad Mapping: The Micro Classic has an AYANEO shortcut that maps the D-pad to emulate a left analog stick. This unlocks N64 games that don't need heavy analog precision: Paper Mario, Mario Party, Pokémon Stadium (mini-games), Kirby 64, Harvest Moon 64. The G99 handles N64 at 2–3× resolution effortlessly.
  4. Android Native Games: TMNT: Shredder's Revenge, Super Meat Boy, Dead Cells, Stardew Valley — all run beautifully with controller support and adapt well to the 3:2 aspect ratio.

⚡ Power User Tips

  • Frontend: ES-DE with a GBA-centric theme (e.g., "RetroBox" or "Minimal"). Use the AYA Window for quick brightness/performance toggles.
  • Battery Management: The 2,600 mAh cell is the device's Achilles' heel. Use Balance Mode for GBA/SNES (5–6 hours) and save Max Mode for when shaders demand it. Carry a small USB-C power bank.
  • Fan Note: The fan is essentially silent below 40°C. For GBA use, it may never spin up audibly.
  • No Headphone Jack: Use a USB-C DAC dongle or Bluetooth 5.2 earbuds. The lack of a jack is a bummer but manageable.
  • Start/Select Quirk: Learn to avoid the home/launcher buttons on the bottom-right chamfer. Consider remapping them with Button Mapper to prevent accidental exits.
🏆 Why This Beats the Others at This Job: The Micro 2 is more powerful but its TMR sticks are wasted on GBA. The KONKR Pocket Advance is plastic and the G90T struggles with heavy shaders. The One35's G85 can't handle CRT-Royale at full speed. The Micro Classic is the only one with the premium build + shader headroom combo.

🥈 Device #2: Ayaneo Pocket Micro 2

🎯 Role: The 6th-Gen Powerhouse — PSP / Dreamcast / GameCube / PS2 / Saturn / Streaming

Why This Role?

The Snapdragon 865 is in a completely different league from the MediaTek chips in the other three. With mature Turnip Vulkan drivers and ~220% of the G99's performance, this is the only device in the collection that can credibly handle GameCube, PS2, Saturn, and high-resolution PSP. The dual recessed TMR sticks (drift-resistant!) are the enabler.

🎨 Creative Assignment: The Analog-Era Portable

  1. Primary Library: PlayStation Portable — PSP at 2× resolution (480×544 internal → 960×544 on a 960×640 panel) fits beautifully. The 3:2 display is closer to 16:9 than 4:3, so PSP's widescreen games have minimal black bars. PPSSPP on SD865 runs at 3×–4× resolution with texture upscaling. Key titles:
    • God of War: Chains of Olympus / Ghost of Sparta — 3×, rock-solid 60 FPS
    • Final Fantasy Tactics: War of the Lions — with HD texture packs
    • Persona 3 Portable, MGS: Peace Walker, Gran Turismo, Ridge Racer
  2. Dreamcast (Flycast / Redream): SD865 runs the entire Dreamcast library at 2–3× resolution with per-pixel sorting. The TMR sticks are perfect for Sonic Adventure, Jet Set Radio, Shenmue, SoulCalibur, Crazy Taxi.
  3. GameCube (Dolphin MMJR2 / official): Most of the library runs at 1×–1.5× resolution. The 3:2 screen handles 4:3 GameCube with modest black bars. Highlights:
    • The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker — 1.5×, widescreen hack
    • Super Mario Sunshine, Metroid Prime, F-Zero GX, Luigi's Mansion, Paper Mario TTYD
    • Use Dolphin MMJR2 for the best SD865 performance profile
  4. PS2 (NetherSX2 / AetherSX2): Cherry-pick titles. Lighter 3D games run well at 1×–1.5×. The heaviest PS2 titles (Shadow of the Colossus, Gran Turismo 4) will struggle. Best bets: Kingdom Hearts I & II, Final Fantasy X, Dragon Quest VIII, Katamari Damacy, Persona 4.
  5. Sega Saturn (Yaba Sanshiro 2 / Beetle Saturn): Saturn emulation is notoriously demanding. The SD865 handles it better than any MediaTek chip. Panzer Dragoon Saga, NiGHTS, Guardian Heroes, Radiant Silvergun.
  6. Game Streaming: WiFi 6 + SD865 = excellent Moonlight/Sunshine streaming from a gaming PC. Xbox Cloud Gaming, Steam Link, and GeForce Now all run smoothly. The 3:2 display and compact size make it a great couch-streaming companion.
  7. N64 at 3×–4×: With the TMR sticks, N64 is native and comfortable. The SD865 can push N64 to high resolutions with texture packs.

⚡ Power User Tips

  • Turnip Drivers: Install Mesa Turnip vulkan drivers (v24+) for Dolphin and NetherSX2. This is the SD865's secret weapon — MediaTek chips can't use these.
  • Thermals: The active fan + SD865 means sustained performance. Use Gaming Mode for GC/PS2 and Max Mode only when needed.
  • Battery: 3,950 mAh gives ~3.5–5.5 hours on heavier 3D emulation, ~5–8 hours on 8/16-bit. Significantly better than the Micro Classic.
  • Video Out: USB-C with video out — you can dock this to a TV and use Bluetooth controllers for a hybrid console experience.
  • 3.5mm Jack: Finally present! Use wired IEMs for zero-latency audio — important for rhythm games and precise platformers.
🏆 Why This Beats the Others at This Job: The Micro Classic and Pocket Advance have no sticks, so GC/PS2/PSP are non-starters. The One35's G85 can barely scrape the surface of GC/PS2. The Micro 2 is the only device here that can run this generation of games and has the sticks to control them.

🎨 Creative Assignment: The Comfort Binge Device

  1. Primary Library: Game Boy Advance (Daily Driver) — While the Micro Classic is the premium GBA experience, the Pocket Advance is the practical one. Its curved body and anti-slip texture make it the most comfortable device for multi-hour GBA sessions. The G90T handles 4× GBA with light shaders (LCD3x, Grid) without breaking a sweat.

    • GBA Multiplayer: Use mGBA's netplay features over WiFi for retroachievements and linked gameplay (Pokémon trading, Four Swords, etc.)
    • RetroAchievements: Enable hardcore mode on this device — the G90T is snappy enough for real-time achievement tracking without lag.
  2. Secondary Library: PlayStation 1 (DuckStation / SwanStation) — The Pocket Advance is the PS1 JRPG machine. The D-pad is perfect for PS1's pre-DualShock library, and the 4,000 mAh battery means you can grind through Final Fantasy VII–IX, Chrono Cross, Xenogears, Suikoden II, Vagrant Story, Legend of Dragoon for hours. Run at 2×–3× resolution with PGXP texture correction for wobbly-polygon-free 3D.
  3. 16-bit & 8-bit Consoles: SNES, Genesis/Mega Drive, NES, TG-16 — the Pocket Advance is the workhorse for these libraries. The volume wheel adds a satisfying tactile element that no other device here has.

⚡ Power User Tips

  • Frontend: Use Daijishō or ES-DE. The Pocket Advance's Android 12 + AYASpace software stack is lighter than Android 13, so use a community launcher.
  • SD Card: The internal storage (32/64 GB) is tight. Use a 256–512 GB microSD for your ROM library. The microSD slot is easily accessible.
  • Volume Wheel: Map the volume wheel carefully — it's a physical analog control, not a rocker. This is genuinely useful for quick volume changes mid-game.
  • Fan: Active cooling via fan. The G90T under load may need it for sustained PS1 upscaling. It's there, use it.
  • The Peach Color: While you said to ignore the Hello Kitty nostalgia, functionally the Peach variant is a matte, warm-toned plastic that may show less fingerprint wear than glossy finishes. It's also the easiest to spot in a bag.
🏆 Why This Beats the Others at This Job: The Micro Classic's metal body is heavier and less comfortable for marathon sessions. The Micro 2 is overkill (and too expensive to casually toss in a bag). The One35's compact shape is less ergonomic. The Pocket Advance is the only one designed with the long-play GBA curve.

🎨 Creative Assignment: The Arcade Cabinets in Your Pocket

  1. Primary Library: Vertical Arcade Shmups (TATE Mode — The Killer App!) — This is why the One35 exists. Rotate 90° and you get a 640×960 vertical canvas — perfect for the 3:4 aspect ratio of classic arcade shooters. The second D-pad + ABXY mapping in Dawn Launcher means you play these in their native orientation:
    • CAVE Classics: DoDonPachi, DoDonPachi DaiOuJou, ESP Ra.De., Ketsui, Mushihimesama, Deathsmiles
    • Psikyo: Strikers 1945 I–III, Gunbird 1 & 2, Dragon Blaze

    • Raizing / 8ing: Battle Garegga, Armed Police Batrider, Soukyugurentai
    • Classics: Galaga, Donkey Kong, Pac-Man, 1942, 1943, Raiden, Twin Cobra
    • Use FinalBurn Neo or MAME cores in RetroArch with Dawn Launcher's per-game TATE orientation preset
  2. Nintendo DS (DraStic — Pre-configured!): The One35 ships with DraStic already set to launch in vertical mode. The two DS screens stack vertically on the rotated display. The side Start/Select buttons become conveniently placed. Best DS experiences:
    • Book-mode games: Brain Age, Hotel Dusk: Room 215, Last Window, Trauma Center
    • Vertical hold games: Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story, The World Ends With You, Ninja Gaiden: Dragon Sword
    • Pokémon (touch-screen heavy): Pokémon Diamond/Pearl/Platinum, HeartGold/SoulSilver, Black/White
    • The 3.5″ display is admittedly small for dual-screen DS. Use the stylus on the touch panel for precision. For games that primarily use one screen, this is perfect.
  3. WonderSwan / WonderSwan Color: The WonderSwan library includes both horizontal and vertical titles. The One35's TATE mode handles vertical WonderSwan games natively: Judgement Silversword, Dicing Knight, Klonoa: Moonlight Museum. The 3:2 screen is also ideal for horizontal WS titles.
  4. PSP (Horizontal Mode): The Helio G85 + Hall-effect analog stick handles PSP at 1×–2× resolution. The 3:2 display fits PSP's aspect ratio better than 4:3 handhelds. Best picks: Lumines, Patapon, LocoRoco, Half-Minute Hero, Persona 3 Portable, Disgaea, Tactics Ogre.
  5. Dreamcast & N64: The single Hall-effect stick is surprisingly good. N64 runs at full speed on the G85 at native resolution. Dreamcast via Flycast/Redream also performs well. Super Mario 64, Ocarina of Time, Sonic Adventure, Crazy Taxi.
  6. Vertical MAME for Classic Arcade: Beyond shmups, many golden-age arcade games use vertical orientation: Donkey Kong, Frogger, BurgerTime, Dig Dug, Mr. Do!, Centipede, Galaxian. The One35 in TATE mode is the most authentic way to play these on any device here.

⚡ Power User Tips

  • Dawn Launcher Orientation: Set per-game orientation in Dawn Launcher. For RetroArch, Dawn bypasses RetroArch's notoriously fiddly rotation settings. This is a first-class feature — use it.
  • TATE Trigger Grip: The top/bottom TATE shoulder buttons use a "claw grip." It takes getting used to. Consider remapping them to less-used functions (rewind, fast-forward, save state) and rely on the face buttons for primary actions.
  • Screen Brightness: The One35 can hit 500–700 nits — the brightest in the group. However, the minimum brightness may be too high for pitch-dark rooms. Use Android's Extra Dim toggle in the quick settings.
  • microSD Caution: The card slot is reportedly tricky — inserting the card upside down can cause it to fall inside the unit. Be careful and insert slowly.
  • Stick Quality: The Hall-effect stick is genuinely good — tight, responsive, and drift-proof. It's better than it has any right to be at this price.
  • Button Mapping: Dawn Launcher includes a touch-to-button mapper for Android games without native controller support — very handy for the One35's unique layout.
🏆 Why This Beats the Others at This Job: No other device here can physically rotate for TATE mode. The Micro Classic, Micro 2, and Pocket Advance are horizontal-only. Playing vertical arcade games letterboxed on a horizontal screen is a sad compromise. The One35 solves this with hardware — it's the only answer for vertical arcade gaming in this collection.

💡 Creative X-Factor Ideas (Beyond the Obvious)

🔴 Micro Classic: The "TV Console Companion"

Since this device lacks sticks, lean all the way into the console experience on a handheld. Set up a dedicated ES-DE collection called "Living Room" with: NES, SNES, Genesis, TG-16, and Neo Geo (AES). Use the CRT-Royale Kurozumi shader preset — it's the most demanding variant and only the G99 (and SD865) can run it smoothly. The Micro Classic becomes a tiny, perfect CRT emulator for console games. The Retro Gold (Famicom) color variant reinforces this theme beautifully.

🔵 Micro 2: The "Docked Party Machine"

The USB-C video out + SD865 + WiFi 6 is a combo no other device here has. Set this up as a TV-dockable retro console: connect to a TV via USB-C → HDMI, pair 2–4 Bluetooth controllers, and run Mario Kart: Double Dash, Super Smash Bros. Melee, Mario Party 4–7, F-Zero GX (GameCube) or Power Stone 2, SoulCalibur, Virtua Tennis (Dreamcast) for multiplayer sessions. The Micro 2 becomes a portable party console.

🟣 Pocket Advance: The "Pokédex" Device

Dedicate this to every mainline Pokémon game playable on GBA + GB/GBC (yes, even though you have the Pocket Vert — this is about the GBA-native experience). The GBA Pokémon games (Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald, FireRed, LeafGreen) fill the 3:2 screen perfectly. Add ROM hacks (Unbound, Glazed, Gaia, Light Platinum) and this becomes a bottomless Pokémon machine. The Pocket Advance's shape even resembles the wide, comfortable GBA that so many of us played Pokémon on.

🟢 One35: The "CAVE Shrine"

Load the One35 with the complete CAVE shmup catalog (MAME/FBNeo) and nothing else. Set up a Dawn Launcher collection called "Vertical Arcade." Every game plays in full TATE glory. This is a dedicated bullet-hell machine — something no other device in the collection can claim. Add RetroAchievements for CAVE games to track 1CC (1-credit clear) progress.

🤔 Things You Might Not Have Considered

1. The Micro Classic Has No Headphone Jack — Plan Around It

The Micro Classic is the only device without a 3.5mm jack. If you're traveling, you'll need either a USB-C DAC or Bluetooth earbuds. The Micro 2, Pocket Advance, and One35 all have headphone jacks. For the Micro Classic specifically, pick up a compact USB-C → 3.5mm dongle (like the Apple USB-C dongle, which has a surprisingly good DAC).

2. The Pocket Advance & One35 Only Have 32–64 GB Internal Storage

Both budget devices use eMMC 5.1, not UFS. This means slower read/write speeds. More importantly, 32–64 GB fills up fast once you add PS1 ISOs or PSP CSOs. Budget for a 256–512 GB microSD card for each (SanDisk Extreme or Samsung EVO Select). The Micro Classic and Micro 2 have 128–256 GB UFS 2.2 built in, which is faster and more spacious.

3. The One35's TATE Mode Is Great — But The Screen Is Small for DS

When you rotate the One35 for DS emulation, each DS screen gets roughly 320×427 pixels (half of the 640×960 rotated canvas). The original DS screens were 256×192. So it's technically bigger, but on a 3.5″ diagonal, the stacked screens are tiny. It works, but it's not ideal for DS games that require reading small text or precise touch input. For DS, consider this a "bonus" rather than the primary use case. The arcade TATE mode is the real star.

4. The Micro 2 Sold Out Extremely Fast — Protect Your Investment

The Micro 2 had extremely limited production runs and sold out in minutes. If you secured one, it's a rare device. Get a good case. AYANEO's production model means replacements or repairs may be difficult. Treat this as a collector's item and a daily driver — but maybe don't toss it in a backpack unprotected.

5. All Four Run Android — Leverage Cross-Device Syncing

Since every device runs Android, you can use Syncthing to keep save files synchronized across the Micro Classic, Micro 2, and Pocket Advance (the One35 runs Android 12 and should also work). Start a GBA game on the Pocket Advance during your commute, continue on the Micro Classic at home on the premium metal body. For RetroArch, sync the saves/ and states/ folders.

6. The "D-pad as Analog Stick" Feature Unlocks N64 on the Micro Classic

AYANEO built in a shortcut that maps the D-pad to emulate a left analog stick. This means the Micro Classic can play N64 games surprisingly well. Games that are primarily D-pad with occasional stick use (like The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time — mostly Z-targeting, or Paper Mario) are very playable. Full analog games like GoldenEye or Perfect Dark will be frustrating. Stick to RPGs, platformers, and puzzle games on N64 for the Micro Classic.

7. The Pocket Advance's G90T GPU Is Actually Better Than the G99's for Some Tasks

This is counterintuitive but worth knowing: the Helio G90T uses a Mali-G76 MC4 GPU, while the G99 uses a Mali-G57 MC2. The G76 has 4 cores vs the G57's 2 cores. For some GPU-bound tasks, the G90T can punch above its weight. However, the G99's CPU cores (Cortex-A76) are much faster than the G90T's (Cortex-A76 at lower clocks + A55), so CPU-heavy emulation (PS2, GC) still favors the G99. For pure GPU shader work, they're closer than you'd think.

8. The One35 Has the Brightest Screen — Outdoor Play Champion

With up to 700 nits peak brightness (some sources say 500 nits sustained), the One35 is the most visible outdoors. If you ever play in direct sunlight (park, beach, bus window), the One35 will be the most usable of the four. The Micro Classic and Micro 2's glass fronts create glare; the Pocket Advance and One35 have less reflective screens.

"The Daily" — GBA and PS1 in a comfortable, affordable, ergonomic package. This is the device you grab for the bus, the waiting room, the lunch break. ROM hacks, RetroAchievements, PS1 JRPGs. The one you're least afraid to use everywhere.

MagicX One35

"The Specialist" — Vertical arcade shmups, NDS in book mode, WonderSwan, PSP Lite. The TATE mode is a genuine hardware differentiator. On the One35, DoDonPachi fills the screen in its native 3:4 orientation. That alone justifies this device's place in the collection.
