KTR1 / KTR2 vs GameMT EX8

A thorough 3:2 retro handheld comparison — with Ayaneo Pocket Ace as the benchmark

Why this comparison matters: The Ayaneo Pocket Ace was (and arguably still is) the best 3:2 Android retro handheld ever made — a Snapdragon G3x Gen 2 powerhouse in a premium 4.5″ 1620×1080 form factor. But it's no longer readily available at retail. For anyone hunting a 3:2 handheld today, the natural alternatives are the KT Pocket KTR1 / KTR2 line and the GameMT EX8. This comparison tells you what you're trading off — and what each device brings to the table.

⚡ Performance & Emulation Capabilities

Ayaneo Pocket Ace — The Performance King

The Snapdragon G3x Gen 2 is essentially a slightly tweaked Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 — one of the most powerful Android chips ever put in a retro handheld. Real-world testing shows:

  • PS2 / GameCube: Full speed at 3× resolution across virtually the entire library. No per-game tinkering needed.
  • Wii: Smooth performance across most titles.
  • Nintendo Switch: Many titles playable (Mario Kart 8, Super Mario Wonder, Mario RPG, Brothership) with occasional graphical glitches. Some heavier titles (Metroid Dread) struggle.
  • PSP: 4×+ resolution with zero issues.
  • Android games: Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail, Diablo Immortal, CoD Mobile all run smoothly even on Balanced mode.
  • Battery life: 12–15 hours on older systems (Balanced mode), ~8 hours on heavier emulation.
  • x86 (Winlator): Light indie PC games run acceptably.

Benchmarks: ~10% behind Odin 2 (Snapdragon 8 Gen 2) in CPU, nearly identical GPU. Peak temperature: 46°C under sustained load.

KTR1 & GameMT EX8 — The Helio G99 Ceiling

Both the KTR1 and EX8 share the MediaTek Helio G99 — a well-understood, proven mid-range chip. The emulation ceiling is well-mapped:

  • 8-bit & 16-bit (NES, SNES, Genesis, GBA): Perfect, full-speed. GBA is a native fit on the 3:2 screen with no letterboxing.
  • PS1: Full library at full speed, 3–4× upscaling.
  • N64: Strong — most of the library runs well at upscaled 1080p.
  • Dreamcast: Good — most titles play at full speed via Flycast at 960p upscale.
  • PSP: Good — most titles run well at 2× resolution. Heaviest titles (God of War) need tuning and may require frameskip.
  • Sega Saturn: Passable — most games run with occasional frame drops; upscaling not recommended.
  • PS2 & GameCube: ⚠️ The ceiling. Only lighter titles are playable, and each requires per-game tweaking (PAL ROMs recommended, underclocking, switching between Vulkan/OpenGL). Frame drops and audio stutter are common. Do not buy for PS2/GC as a primary use case.
  • 3DS: Hit or miss — lighter titles playable with compromises.
  • Switch / Wii U: Not supported.

Both handhelds include active cooling, which helps sustain the Helio G99's performance without thermal throttling during long sessions — a genuine advantage over passively cooled G99 handhelds.

KTR2 — The Dimensity 7300 Wildcard

The KTR2's MediaTek Dimensity 7300 (2024, 4nm) represents a genuine step up from the Helio G99:

  • 4× Cortex-A78 (up to 2.5 GHz) + 4× Cortex-A55 (2.0 GHz) vs. the G99's 2× A76 + 6× A55
  • Mali-G615 MP2 GPU — newer architecture, better efficiency
  • Expected to sit between the Helio G99 and Snapdragon G3x Gen 2 in real-world emulation
  • PS2 / GameCube should be significantly more viable than on the G99, though still not at Ace/Odin levels
  • Retroshelf rates it as supporting "up to 3DS" at excellent quality, with Switch listed as "limited"
⚠️ Important caveat: The KTR2 has no independent reviews yet. All performance expectations are based on the Dimensity 7300's on-paper specs and KT Pocket's claims. Real-world performance is unverified. The KTR1 had similar promising specs but disappointed on PS2/GC in practice.

📐 The 3:2 Screen Story

All four devices share the same fundamental display concept: a 3:2 aspect ratio at 1620×1080 resolution — a panel type that the KTR1 originally pioneered and that Ayaneo later used for the Pocket Ace. According to Retro Handhelds' review, the Ace screen is "essentially the same panel as the KTR1."

Why 3:2?

  • GBA: Native 3:2 — the perfect fit. No letterboxing, no pillarboxing. This is the aspect ratio's killer app.
  • 4:3 systems (SNES, Genesis, PS1, GameCube, PS2): Small black bars on left/right — much less intrusive than 16:9 letterboxing.
  • 16:9 systems (PSP, modern streaming): Black bars on top/bottom — acceptable and common on all non-widescreen handhelds.
  • It's the best compromise aspect ratio for playing both retro 4:3 and modern 16:9 content on one device.

🏗️ Build Quality & Reliability

Ayaneo Pocket Ace — Premium, But Not Perfect

✅ Pros

  • Excellent fit & finish; one of the best-built retro handhelds
  • Best D-pad AYANEO has ever made
  • Hall-effect sticks & triggers feel great
  • Quiet, satisfying face buttons
  • Front-facing speakers — great clarity and bass
  • Fingerprint sensor in power button

❌ Cons

  • Light bleed on the IPS panel in low-light
  • No headphone jack
  • Tiny analog sticks — hard to use for modern games
  • Fan gets loud at max speed
  • No charger included in box
  • AYASpace software is still "tolerable" at best

KTR1 — Ambitious But Flawed

✅ Pros

  • Configurable D-pad/stick layout
  • Front-firing stereo speakers
  • Optional aluminum shell
  • Large battery options
  • Customizable colors

❌ Cons

  • Major QC issues: Screens arriving detached, poor assembly
  • Flimsy shoulder triggers — lack crisp click, can rattle
  • D-pad described as "wonky" by multiple reviewers
  • Screen too dim for outdoor use
  • Small thumbsticks with limited travel
  • Extremely long wait times from order to delivery
  • One-person operation — thin documentation, uncertain support
  • No video output

KTR2 — Promising On Paper, Unproven

The KTR2 addresses some KTR1 criticisms: magnesium alloy option, Hall-effect joysticks, updated chipset, and design refinements. But KT Pocket remains a tiny operation with a poor track record:

  • KTR1 launch was "a disaster, poorly launched and poorly crafted" — RetroDodo
  • Pre-sale requires a deposit ($59 plastic / $89 magnesium) with balance due "10 days before shipping"
  • Shipments expected Feb–Mar 2026 but KT Pocket has a history of delays
  • No independent reviews exist — all specs are from KT Pocket's marketing
  • International availability unclear; currently China-focused
⚠️ Our recommendation: Do not pre-order the KTR2 until independent reviews confirm quality and performance. The KTR1 taught us that specs on paper mean nothing if the device arrives broken or never ships.

GameMT EX8 — Budget Build, Surprising Execution

✅ Pros

  • Better plastic than GameMT's earlier devices
  • Glass front panel — looks cleaner than price suggests
  • Blocky GBA-inspired design — comfortable and pocketable
  • Hall-effect joysticks with RGB rings
  • Active cooling (20,000 RPM fan) — rare at this price
  • 6-axis gyro + linear haptic motor
  • Two nice colorways: Black/Red and White/Purple

❌ Cons

  • D-pad input quirks reported on some units — bad for fighting games/platformers
  • Smaller brand with shorter support track record
  • Bottom-firing speakers (vs. front-firing on Ace/KTR1)
  • No video output
  • Only one RAM/storage config (6GB/128GB)
  • Plastic shell only — no metal option

🎯 Who Should Buy What?

🟢 Buy the GameMT EX8 if…

  • You want a 3:2 handheld available right now at a great price
  • Your emulation needs top out at PSP / Dreamcast / N64
  • You're okay with an LCD (not OLED) and can accept the slight risk of d-pad quirks
  • You want the largest 3:2 screen in this comparison (4.88″)
  • You're on a budget but want active cooling and Hall sticks

🟡 Wait for the KTR2 if…

  • You can be patient (shipments not expected until mid-2026 and may be delayed further)
  • You want a step up from the Helio G99 without paying Ace prices
  • You value the magnesium alloy build and premium feel
  • You're willing to take a risk on a small, unproven brand
  • But wait for independent reviews first!

🔴 Try to find an Ayaneo Pocket Ace (or alternative) if…

  • You want PS2 / GameCube / Wii / Switch emulation without compromises
  • You want premium build quality, excellent controls, and a polished experience
  • You're willing to hunt one down on the secondary market or pay a premium
  • Alternative: Consider the AYN Odin 2 Mini ($349) or Retroid Pocket 6 — both offer flagship performance without the 3:2 screen but with better availability

⚫ Skip the KTR1 entirely…

  • Unless you find one used at a steep discount and accept the QC risks
  • The EX8 delivers the same Helio G99 performance with better availability, a larger screen, and a lower price
  • The KTR1 was, as one reviewer put it, "an overpriced PSP emulator" at its original MSRP

🔮 The Bottom Line

🏆 Best Overall 3:2 Handheld (if you can find it): Ayaneo Pocket Ace

The Ace remains the gold standard — Snapdragon G3x Gen 2 performance, premium build, fantastic controls, and that lovely 3:2 screen. But it's scarce and expensive. If you see one at a reasonable price, grab it.

🥇 Best Available-Now 3:2 Handheld: GameMT EX8

At $140, the EX8 is the clear winner for anyone who can't find an Ace. Same chip as the KTR1 and Pocket Micro, but with a bigger 4.88″ screen, active cooling, Hall sticks, and it's actually in stock. The d-pad quirks are the only real asterisk — check recent buyer reports if you play a lot of fighting games or precision platformers. For everything up through PSP and Dreamcast, it's a genuine budget sleeper.

⏳ The KTR2 Is Intriguing, But Wait

The Dimensity 7300 could make the KTR2 a compelling mid-range 3:2 option — better than the G99 but far cheaper than the Ace. But KT Pocket must prove itself first. The KTR1's disastrous launch overshadows everything. Wait for real reviews, real shipping dates, and real buyers confirming quality before putting money down.
