The Game Boy Advance (released 2001) was Nintendo's final handheld to carry the Game Boy name and perhaps its greatest. Often described as "a portable SNES," the GBA punched far above its weight with a stunning library spanning original IP, SNES-era ports, and genre-defining classics that still hold up today.

What follows is a thoroughly researched guide to the essential GBA games — the titles that critics, fans, and historians consistently point to as the defining experiences of the platform. This list draws from Metacritic's all-time rankings, Nintendo Life's 50 Best GBA Games, community user scores, and widely cited retrospective sources.

Note: Scores from Metacritic where available. Original cartridge prices are approximate market values as of 2025.

👑 Tier S — The Absolute Essentials

These are the games that define the GBA. If you only play a handful of GBA games, make it these. Universally acclaimed, endlessly replayable, and inseparable from the identity of the platform.

2004 Nintendo R&D1 MC 89

Metroid: Zero Mission

Nintendo Life's #1 GBA game of all time. A from-the-ground-up remake of the 1986 NES original, rebuilt with Super Metroid-inspired gameplay, gorgeous visuals, save rooms, new areas, mini-bosses, and an entirely new post-Mother Brain stealth section where Samus must survive in her Zero Suit. Widely considered one of the greatest remakes in gaming history — some even prefer it over Super Metroid.

2002 Nintendo R&D1 MC 92

Metroid Fusion

"Metroid 4" — a bold continuation of Samus's story after Super Metroid. More linear than its predecessor, but that linearity serves a masterful horror-tinged atmosphere. The SA-X — an unkillable clone of Samus at full power — stalks you through the BSL research station in genuinely terrifying sequences. The game that directly inspired Metroid Dread. Metacritic score of 92 — among the highest on the platform.

2003 Konami (KCEK) MC 91

Castlevania: Aria of Sorrow

The crown jewel of the GBA Castlevania trilogy and the game many fans consider second only to Symphony of the Night in the entire series. Set in 2035, it introduces the brilliant "Soul System" — absorb enemy souls to gain their abilities, offering staggering replayability. The sheer number of weapons, items, and souls to collect is remarkable for a handheld title. Included in the Castlevania Advance Collection on modern platforms.

2005 Game Freak User 8.8

Pokémon Emerald

The definitive Gen III experience. An enhanced version of Ruby & Sapphire featuring both Team Magma and Team Aqua as antagonists, animated Pokémon sprites in battle, and the legendary Battle Frontier — a massive post-game complex with seven unique battle facilities. After beating the Elite Four, you can rematch Gym Leaders. Rayquaza mediates the clash between Kyogre and Groudon in an epic conclusion. The Hoenn region never looked better.

2005 Capcom / Flagship MC 89

The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap

The GBA's lone original single-player Zelda — and what a gem. Developed by Capcom (who also made the excellent Oracle games), Link gains the ability to shrink to microscopic size using Ezlo, a talking cap (and cursed Minish sage). The world is absolutely packed with secrets — Kinstone fusions, hidden Minish dwellings, sword techniques to learn. Gorgeous sprite work, wonderful dungeon design, and arguably the best-looking 2D Zelda ever made.

2001 Intelligent Systems MC 92

Advance Wars

The turn-based tactics game that defined a generation. So beloved that it spawned a franchise and a recent Switch remake. Command ground, air, and naval forces across 114 maps with charming pixel art and an infectiously catchy soundtrack. The game that proved strategy could work beautifully on a handheld. Perfectly balanced, deeply satisfying, and one of the highest-rated GBA games at 92 Metascore. The CO system (Commanding Officers with unique powers) adds incredible strategic depth.

⭐ Tier A — Platform-Defining Classics

These are the games that any serious GBA collection must include. They represent the platform at its finest and are frequently cited as among the best games ever made, regardless of platform.

2004 Game Freak User 8.6

Pokémon FireRed & LeafGreen

The definitive way to revisit Kanto. These remakes of the original Red & Blue brought the first generation into the modern era with GBA visuals, the Sevii Islands post-game, wireless adapter support, and the ability to connect with Ruby/Sapphire/Emerald to complete the National Pokédex (350+ Pokémon). For millions of fans, this is Pokémon at its purest. Now also available on Nintendo Switch.

2001 Camelot MC 91

Golden Sun / The Lost Age

The original GBA RPG saga. Camelot's masterpiece introduced the Djinn system — collectible elemental creatures that modify your characters' classes and unleash powerful summons. A gripping story about the release of Alchemy into the world of Weyard, clever Psynergy-based puzzles, and some of the best 2D graphics on the system. The Lost Age continues and concludes the story. Both are available on Nintendo Switch Online.

2003 AlphaDream MC 90

Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga

The debut of the beloved Mario & Luigi RPG series. A hilarious adventure through the Beanbean Kingdom with ingenious dual-brother gameplay: control both Mario and Luigi simultaneously on the overworld and in a timing-based battle system that rewards skillful play. Fawful is one of the most memorable villains Nintendo has ever created. The writing is genuinely funny, the animations are full of charm, and the Bros. Attacks are spectacular.

2003 Intelligent Systems MC 88

Fire Emblem (The Blazing Blade)

The game that introduced Fire Emblem to the West. After Roy and Marth appeared in Super Smash Bros. Melee, Western fans finally got to experience the tactical RPG series. This prequel follows Eliwood (Roy's father), Hector, and Lyn across the continent of Elibe. Permadeath, weapon triangles, support conversations, and branching story paths. With 8.9 user score on Metacritic, it remains one of the most beloved entries and the perfect introduction to the franchise.

2003 Nintendo R&D1 MC 89

WarioWare, Inc.: Mega Microgame$!

The game that invented the microgame genre. Over 200 rapid-fire mini-games lasting just 3–5 seconds each, stitched together into an increasingly frantic fever dream. Nose-picking, jump-roping, dodging traffic — you never know what's coming next. Pure, distilled Nintendo creativity and one of the most uniquely GBA experiences imaginable. The series continues to this day on Switch.

2003 Square MC 87

Final Fantasy Tactics Advance

A tactical RPG that some players sank 200+ hours into. Build a clan, master jobs, and battle across the fantasy world of Ivalice. The "Law" system adds a unique wrinkle — each battle has rules that restrict certain actions, forcing constant adaptation. The story follows children transported into a magical version of their hometown via an ancient book. Deeply addictive and one of the most replayable strategy games on any system.

2001 Intelligent Systems MC 93

Mario Kart: Super Circuit

The highest-rated Mario Kart on Metacritic until Mario Kart 8 Deluxe. 40 tracks (20 new + all 20 from the SNES original), tight drifting mechanics, and the series' signature item-based chaos — all on a handheld. Four-player link cable multiplayer made this a schoolyard essential. The fact that it still plays beautifully today at 93 Metascore is a testament to its quality.

2002 Nintendo MC 95

The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords

The highest-rated GBA game on Metacritic with an incredible 95. The definitive 2D Zelda experience, brought to handheld with added voice samples, tweaked gameplay, and — most importantly — the brand-new Four Swords multiplayer adventure. Up to four players could link up for a cooperative (and competitive) Zelda dungeon crawl. Two legendary games in one cartridge.

💎 Tier B — Must-Play Gems & Cult Classics

These games might not always be the first named in a "top 10," but they're absolutely essential to understanding what made the GBA special. Many are cult classics that hardcore fans treasure.

2001 Nintendo R&D1 MC 88

Wario Land 4

A platforming masterclass. Non-linear levels that require exploration and multiple playthroughs, Wario's unique charging/rushing mechanics, and a surreal audiovisual style that paved the way for the WarioWare series. Each level ends with a frantic escape sequence after hitting the switch. Short but dense with content — quality over quantity at its finest. User score of 8.8 on Metacritic.

2001 Konami (KCEK) MC 91

Castlevania: Circle of the Moon

A GBA launch title and the first handheld "Metroidvania." Though notoriously dark on the original unlit GBA screen, it shines on modern backlit displays. The DSS (Dual Set-up System) card-combination magic system offers incredible variety. A challenging, atmospheric action-RPG that proved the GBA could handle Symphony of the Night-style gameplay. Metascore of 91 — tied for the highest among GBA Castlevanias.

2006 Brownie Brown / HAL Cult Classic

Mother 3

The legendary Japan-only sequel to EarthBound. A game so beloved that fans created their own professional-quality English translation patch. Follows Lucas (of Super Smash Bros. Brawl fame) through an emotionally devastating story about family, loss, and resisting an invading "Pig Mask Army." Features a rhythm-based battle system where tapping to the beat of the music deals extra damage. A masterwork of RPG storytelling that Nintendo has inexplicably never localized. Available on Japanese Nintendo Switch Online.

2007 TOSE / Square Enix MC 92

Final Fantasy VI Advance

Often cited as one of the greatest RPGs of all time, made portable. The GBA version features a re-translated script, new dungeons, new Espers (summons), and new superbosses. The only downside is slightly compressed music quality compared to the SNES original. Kefka remains one of gaming's most memorable villains, and the opera scene is legendary. A 92 Metascore confirms its status as essential.

2002 HAL Laboratory User 8.4

Kirby: Nightmare in Dream Land

A delightful remake of the NES classic Kirby's Adventure with completely redrawn visuals, vibrant colors, and the addition of Meta Knightmare mode (play through the entire game as Meta Knight). The game that introduced Kirby's copy abilities to the world, now in gorgeous 32-bit spritework. Charming, accessible, and perfect for players of all ages. The soundtrack — especially "Green Greens" and "Yogurt Yard" — is pure joy.

2004 Treasure / Hitmaker Cult Classic

Astro Boy: Omega Factor

Treasure's GBA masterpiece. A stunning blend of beat-'em-up, platforming, and shoot-'em-up stages, all rendered with gorgeous spritework. Huge bosses, a deep (and surprisingly emotional) story that spans multiple playthroughs, and tight combat with Astro's laser cannon, drill attacks, and machine guns. One of the best-looking games on the system and proof that licensed games can be exceptional.

2005 Game Freak Hidden Gem

Drill Dozer

Game Freak's non-Pokémon triumph. A drill-based action-platformer with a rumble cartridge — the GBA cart literally vibrates as you drill through blocks and enemies. Tight level design, clever puzzles, hidden treasures, and satisfying boss battles. Despite critical acclaim (it won GBA Game of the Year awards), it was a commercial disappointment, making it a prized collector's item today ($80–120 for authentic copies).

2002 Nintendo MC 91

Yoshi's Island: Super Mario Advance 3

The GBA port of what many consider the greatest 2D platformer ever made. The pastel storybook art style, Yoshi's flutter jump and egg-throwing mechanics, and that unforgettable final boss fight against Baby Bowser. The smaller screen slightly reduces the impact of the SNES original's visuals, but the core gameplay is timeless. 100% completion is a genuine challenge requiring mastery of every level. Metascore of 91, user score of 8.9.

2002 Konami (KCEK) MC 87

Castlevania: Harmony of Dissonance

The middle child of the GBA Castlevania trilogy and the first produced by Koji Igarashi himself, with art by Symphony of the Night illustrator Ayami Kojima. Features a dual-castle system where you warp between two versions of Dracula's castle. Deliberately brighter than Circle of the Moon to compensate for the original GBA's dark screen. Juste Belmont's spell-book + sub-weapon combination system offers impressive variety. Included in the Castlevania Advance Collection.

2002 Dimps / Sonic Team Essential Platformer

Sonic Advance

The first Sonic game on a Nintendo console — a landmark moment in gaming history. Developer Dimps absolutely nailed the classic Genesis physics and sense of speed while adding fresh elements. Four playable characters (Sonic, Tails, Knuckles, Amy), vibrant visuals, a Tiny Chao Garden that linked to Sonic Adventure 2: Battle on GameCube, and multiplayer via link cable. The game that proved Sonic could thrive on Nintendo hardware.

2004 Inti Creates Series Peak

Mega Man Zero 3

The peak of the Mega Man Zero quadrilogy on GBA. Refined gameplay from the first two entries, the "Recoil Rod" weapon, Cyber-Elf system, and a satisfying story. Zero wields the Z-Saber, Buster Shot, Shield Boomerang, and Recoil Rod through challenging levels with tough-as-nails boss fights. The Zero series is notably harder than the classic Mega Man games. The entire series is available in the Mega Man Zero/ZX Legacy Collection.

2005 Intelligent Systems MC 85

Fire Emblem: The Sacred Stones

The GBA's Fire Emblem swansong. Stars royal twins Eirika and Ephraim in a branching story — choose which sibling's path to follow. Introduced a traversable world map, monster battles in the Tower of Valni for grinding, and class promotion branches. Slightly easier than Blazing Blade, making it a great entry point. The GBA Fire Emblem sprite animations remain arguably the best the series has ever looked.
