Buying brief · PS1-and-below handheld · researched 3 Nov 2026 · US$ unless stated

RG476H vs RG477M

Both use the same 4.7-inch 1280×960 120Hz LCD. The choice comes down to how much shader budget you want to pay for, and what you give up to get it.

Buy the RG476H, provided crt-geom-mini holds up in your RG Cube test

Your Pocket S Mini already covers PS2 and up, so most of the RG477M's extra speed would duplicate it. The 476H is lighter, quieter and has the better shoulder setup. You pay less, though the gap can shrink a lot during sales.

Switch to the RG477M if you decide you want crt-geom-deluxe with BFI, or plan to use crt-beam-simulator with a heavy CRT shader. That is the one job here the T820 probably can't do.

What each one is better at

RG476H: pros

  • About 65 g lighter, at 290 g
  • Quiet fan that reviewers rarely notice
  • Stacked shoulders with analog Hall triggers
  • Near-bezel-less glass front
  • Often about $100 cheaper at list price
  • Long battery life: 14 h on non-3D retro in one test

RG476H: cons

  • Far less GPU headroom for shaders and upscaling
  • Reviewers point to light CRT shaders
  • One long-term review calls Saturn "quite compromised"
  • Face buttons felt stiff to one reviewer
  • Glass front picks up fingerprints

RG477M: pros

  • About 4x the GPU score in the closest benchmark
  • Reported to run crt-geom-deluxe
  • Room to combine upscaling, shaders and BFI
  • PS1, Dreamcast, N64 and Saturn run effortlessly
  • Metal shell with a premium feel
  • Newer stock Android and Wi-Fi 6E

RG477M: cons

  • Whiny fan is its most repeated complaint
  • Heavy at 354–357 g, with one review calling it an "indoor handheld"
  • Digital inline triggers
  • Shell warms up, and it's more exposed to drop damage
  • Tall sticks snag in bags
  • Plain flat back with weak grips

Things you might miss

BFI has a recurring flicker on GammaOS

Zelpio reports a periodic flicker about every 30 seconds to prevent image retention on the 477M. Expect similar behaviour on the same panel, and try it during your return window.

BFI may feel subtle on this LCD

One long-term 477M review calls the change small at first, but hard to give up later. The same reviewer says a good shader without BFI already looks great.

The Mali driver question affects both

Both chips use Mali GPUs, without the Turnip driver route Snapdragon has. A reviewer of the RG477V, which shares the 477M's chip, recommends Vulkan in RetroArch for BFI and run-ahead. I found no equivalent note for the T820. Dolphin may need swapping between Vulkan and OpenGL.

Video out reports conflict on the 476H

Joey says it has no video output. Retro Handhelds' spec list, a buyer report and GammaOS's docking mode all point to USB-C output. One buyer says you can't charge at the same time.

Save access is awkward on stock firmware

Joey notes restricted access to Android's data folders, which complicates save transfers for some standalone emulators. RetroArch can work around it. GammaOS relaxes these limits, which matters if you sync saves.

GammaOS flashing has real costs

The flash wipes the device and the 476H release is documented as Windows-only. It was Patreon-first, with public release scheduled for February 2026, so my earlier note made it sound more restricted than it should be now.

Bloat comes with both

Reviewers criticise the launcher and the bundled Anbernic AI on each. Android Authority also found outdated emulators on its 477M. Plan to replace these with your own frontend and RetroArch.

Netflix games and DRM

Forum buyers report that Netflix games work on the 476H but not the 477M, possibly because of Widevine. This is anecdotal, and only matters if you use them.

Australian retail is a very different price

Electro Arcade lists the 476H at A$328, far above AliExpress pricing. Check the landed price, warranty handling and return cost. I found no current AUD 477M price.

Sales can narrow the gap

The best prices I saw were about $62 apart, not $100+. AliExpress 11.11 is running now, so compare both on the same day. If the gap is small, the decision should turn on weight and fan noise.

Before you pay

  1. Run crt-geom-mini on your RG Cube with a PS1 game at your intended internal resolution. It has the same T820.
  2. Scale the shader cost by about 2.4x. The 476H has that many more pixels than the Cube's 720×720 screen.
  3. Check it against my rough limits. Under 5–6 ms on the Cube suits classic BFI. Under 3 ms is needed for a shader on every 120Hz sub-frame. These limits are estimates, not published figures.
  4. Test a Saturn game too if you play them, since this is where 476H reviews disagree.

Biggest gap in the evidence
I found no frame-time measurements for specific shaders with BFI on either device, so the shader verdict rests on review impressions, GPU ratios and my own estimates. Your Cube test will tell you more than any of the reviews here.
