Pedal clone lineages: ten famous circuits and everything that copied them

Most clones change one part on purpose: a clipping diode, an op-amp or a transistor. That one part is usually what the whole argument is about. The famous originals often began as copies too: the Tube Screamer descends from the Boss OD-1, the Ross from the Dyna Comp, and the Tone Bender from the Maestro Fuzz-Tone.

Three parts decide most of the "it's not the same" arguments

Simple circuits have few parts, so swapping one of them changes a lot. These are the swaps that keep coming up in every family below.

Clipping diodes

Germanium clips early and soft. Silicon clips harder at about 0.7 V. Red LEDs clip at about 2 V, which means more output and less compression. Asymmetric pairs add even harmonics. This one difference separates the Klon from the Soul Food, the Distortion+ from the DOD 250, and the RAT from the Turbo RAT.

Op-amp

The JRC4558 in the Tube Screamer, the LM308 in the RAT, the TL072 in the Klon and the LM741 in the DOD 250. Slew rate and noise matter most when the chip itself is part of the gain stage, as in the RAT. In most other circuits the difference is smaller than forums suggest.

Transistors

Germanium (NKT275, AC128, OC44, OC75) against silicon (BC108, BC109). Germanium is warmer, varies a lot from part to part and is sensitive to heat. Silicon is brighter, has more gain and is consistent. This is the main fault line in every fuzz family.

How to read the "kind" labels

Revision The original maker changed its own circuit
Reissue The rights holder rebuilt an old version
Rebadge The same circuit sold under another brand
Clone A third party copied the circuit closely
Mod An existing unit was altered
Derivative Built on the same topology but voiced differently, like the King of Tone

Patterns across the families

"The original" is often more than one circuit. EHX's own historian says there was never one Triangle or Ram's Head schematic, only dozens of variants. Fuzz Faces changed transistors partway through production. Tube Screamers shipped with several different chips. When someone clones "a Ram's Head", they are really cloning one particular unit.

Patents and cost drive changes as much as tone does. According to Ibanez's former product manager, Boss's patent on asymmetric clipping is a likely reason the TS808 clips symmetrically. Arbiter moved to silicon because it was cheaper and more stable. ProCo moved to the OP07 because the LM308 was hard to get.

Scarcity creates the clone market. The Klon, Rangemaster and early Bluesbreaker all became expensive because few were made or because the parts ran out. Klones only boomed after Centaur supply dried up.

Derivatives often outsell their source. The Morning Glory and King of Tone are better known than the 1991 Marshall pedal they came from. Many builders now treat the Ross, not the Dyna Comp, as the reference compressor.
