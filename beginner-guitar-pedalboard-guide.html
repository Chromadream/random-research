A first pedalboard: four pedals, one order, clean power

Tuner, a drive, a delay and a reverb cover most of what a beginner needs. Put them in the order below, power them properly, and swap any slot for something stranger once the basics feel boring.

Go mini: the same board in about half the space

Mini pedals are roughly half the width of a standard stompbox, usually with three knobs and one footswitch. Most sound the same as the full-size version, and a seven-pedal mini board fits on a board you can carry in one hand. Every slot has a good mini option now.

What you give up

  • No battery compartment: they all need a power supply, so budget for one from day one.
  • Fewer controls. Extra features hide behind internal trim pots, toggles on the side, or an app.
  • Footswitches sit close together; easy to stomp the wrong one. Fix them down firmly.

Power is the pedal nobody mentions, and the usual cause of hum

Every pedal needs the right voltage (usually 9V), the right polarity (almost always centre-negative) and at least as much current as it draws. Extra current capacity is fine; too little causes noise and glitches. Digital delays, reverbs and modelers draw far more than a drive pedal.

Daisy chainOne adapter, one split cable. Fine for two or three simple analog pedals. Cheap.
Isolated brickEach output has its own power and ground, so one pedal's noise can't leak into another. Buy this once.
BatteriesWork, die mid-song, and most mini pedals have no room for one. Avoid.

Plumbing checklist

  • Isolated supplies to look at: Truetone 1 Spot Pro CS6, Voodoo Lab Iso-5 or Pedal Power 3, Strymon Zuma; budget isolated bricks from Donner, Joyo and Caline are fine to start.
  • Add up each pedal's mA (printed near the jack) and check every output can cover it. Digital pedals often want 250–500 mA.
  • Short, decent patch cables. Flat "pancake" plugs let pedals sit closer. Bad cables cause more crackle than bad pedals.
  • A board with hook-and-loop tape. Any flat plank works; the point is that nothing moves and you can reorder freely.
  • Keep power cables away from (or crossing at right angles to) audio cables.
  • Amp with its own distortion and an effects loop? Drives go in front of the amp; modulation, delay and reverb can sit in the loop so they don't get smeared by the amp's gain.
