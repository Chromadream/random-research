4. Blu-ray–Specific Technical Considerations (Read This)

These are the things a movie-only, Blu-ray buyer needs to know that most AR-glasses reviews don't cover.

✅ HDCP — will protected discs play?

Yes, on RayNeo. RayNeo's official support page states the Air glasses, Pocket TV, and the HDMI→USB-C adapter "support the HDCP protocol, allowing you to watch licensed content." This is essential: without HDCP, a Blu-ray player sends only a black screen. Third-party adapters vary, so sticking with the official RayNeo adapter for a Blu-ray source is the safest path.

✅ 4K UHD Blu-ray → 1080p downscale

All candidates here are 1080p panels. A 4K Blu-ray is downscaled to 1080p by the chain (adapter + glasses). You still get the HDR10 color/contrast benefit, but not 4K sharpness. Since you said 4K isn't required, this is fine — but set expectations: you are not seeing "4K."

✅ HDR10 is the right format for discs

4K UHD Blu-ray's baseline HDR format is HDR10 (10-bit). The RayNeo adapter passes HDR10 and the Air 4 Pro decodes HDR10 natively. (Dolby Vision discs will fall back to the HDR10 base layer — also fine.) Full HD Blu-rays are SDR; the Air 4 Pro's AI-HDR mode will upconvert them to a punchier look if you want it.

⚠️ 24p / cinema frame rate

Blu-rays output 1080p24 (24 fps). The adapter's listed modes are 1080p@120Hz and 4K@30Hz; 24Hz passthrough isn't explicitly documented. In practice this is handled one of two ways: the player outputs 24p within a 60Hz-capable HDMI 2.0 link (fine), or the source does 3:2 pulldown to 60Hz. You may see minor judder on slow pans if 24Hz isn't preserved. This is a generic caveat for nearly all AR glasses — none of them are marketed as 24p-cinema displays.

⚠️ 3D Blu-ray discs won't play directly

3D Blu-ray uses MVC frame-packing, which these glasses cannot decode — they only accept side-by-side (SBS) or top-bottom 3D (3840×1080 SBS on the Air 4 Pro). So commercial 3D Blu-ray discs will not display in 3D through the adapter. You'd need to rip/convert the disc to SBS on a PC first. Regular 2D Blu-rays are unaffected.

🔊 Audio is downmixed to stereo

The glasses receive stereo PCM over USB-C. Dolby Atmos / DTS:X / TrueHD / DTS-HD MA from the disc are downmixed to 2-channel. For a "movie on your face" this is expected and generally sounds good (the Air 4 Pro's B&O quad speakers are well-reviewed), but don't expect true surround. For headphones, pair Bluetooth to the player or another device, not the glasses.

HDR10 caveats on the Air 4 Pro (worth knowing before you buy):
  • HDR10 mode locks the glasses to 60Hz (120Hz only in SDR/AI-HDR). Irrelevant for 24 fps movies.
  • Android Police reported occasional flicker in HDR10 mode; PCMag and Tom's Guide both note HDR10 vs. AI-HDR differences are subtle in real viewing.
  • HDR10 requires an actual HDR10 source signal — which a 4K UHD Blu-ray player provides, so your chain qualifies.

6. Recommended Setup & Buying Path

  1. Buy the RayNeo Air 4 Pro + HDMI Adapter bundle ($339) — this is the exact pairing you're asking about, officially validated together.
  2. Connect: Blu-ray player → HDMI cable → RayNeo HDMI 2.0 adapter → USB-C → Air 4 Pro. The adapter's built-in battery means the glasses are powered without draining anything.
