TrimUI Brick vs Miyoo Mini Plus vs Anbernic RG Rotate

The ultimate daily-carry upgrade comparison for someone who already owns an original Miyoo Mini — with save-sync, USB DAC, and non-gaming considerations.

🏆 The Short Answer

For your specific needs (puzzle games, incremental RPGs, save sync, USB DAC, non-gaming, daily carry):

🥇 Anbernic RG Rotate (Silver) — best overall fit: Android means effortless Syncthing saves, native USB-C DAC support, full DAP/music player capabilities, unique form factor.
🥈 TrimUI Brick / Brick Hammer — best screen quality and build; save-sync possible but fiddlier; USB DAC support depends on CFW.
🥉 Miyoo Mini Plus — the "safe" pick with the best CFW (Onion OS), but lowest specs and least differentiation from your existing Miyoo Mini.

🧱 TrimUI Brick & Brick Hammer

The Screen Is the Star

The 3.2" 1024×768 IPS display is the standout feature. At ~400 PPI, it's the sharpest screen in this price bracket — sharper even than many premium handhelds. For puzzle games with fine details (think Picross, Panel de Pon) and RPG text, this display is a genuine pleasure. The high pixel density means non-integer scaling still looks clean — you spend less time fiddling with shaders and more time playing.

The Brick's screen is notably brighter and sharper than the Miyoo Mini Plus display. Multiple reviewers describe the Brick as making the MM+ screen look dated when held side-by-side.

Brick vs Brick Hammer — What's the Difference?

The Brick Hammer is the same device internally, but replaces the plastic shell with a full CNC aluminum body — think of it as the "premium feel" edition. It adds about 31–40 g of weight (192–199 g vs 159 g for plastic). The Hammer also has nicer thermal characteristics since the metal body spreads heat more evenly. For daily carry, the plastic Brick is arguably better — lighter and cheaper. The Hammer is for those who prioritize feel over practicality.

Emulation Ceiling

Both Brick models top out around PS1 with some light N64/Dreamcast in "bonus territory." Performance is identical to the TrimUI Smart Pro. For puzzles (mostly 8-bit/16-bit/GBA) and retro RPGs (SNES, PS1), this is more than enough. The lack of analog sticks is actually a plus for pocketability — nothing to snag.

🍄 Miyoo Mini Plus

The Community Darling — But Is It Different Enough?

You already own the original Miyoo Mini. The Plus is essentially that device with a larger 3.5" screen, Wi-Fi, and a bigger battery. The internals are nearly identical — same ARM Cortex-A7, same 128 MB RAM, same emulation ceiling (up to PS1). For someone looking for an upgrade, the Plus doesn't move the needle enough.

Where the Plus shines is Onion OS — still arguably the most polished, mature CFW in the hobby. Game Switcher, activity tracker, RetroAchievements, and the vast theme ecosystem are genuinely delightful. But you can already experience most of this on your existing Miyoo Mini.

Non-Gaming Capabilities

Onion OS offers the GMU music player (MP3, FLAC, OGG) and the Pixel Reader ebook app (epub, txt). There's also the excellent community-built MiyooPod app — a full iPod-inspired MP3 player with album art, lyrics, and theme support. For casual music listening and reading, it works. But editing config files for GMU keybinds and managing playlists is decidedly not user-friendly compared to Android alternatives.

Save Sync

Syncthing is not natively supported on Onion OS — it requires a manual installation via a community package. Once set up, it works, but the process is fragile across Onion OS updates. Multiple users report that the Syncthing setup on Miyoo devices is "advanced" territory.

🔁 Anbernic RG Rotate (Aurora Silver)

The Wildcard — and the Best Fit for Your Needs

The RG Rotate is fundamentally different: a square 3.5" 720×720 display on a physical swivel hinge that reveals the controls underneath. It runs full Android 12 with Play Store access. The Aurora Silver version uses a full aluminum body (204 g) that feels genuinely premium — multiple reviewers compared the hinge feel to high-end flip phones.

For puzzle games and retro RPGs, the square screen is surprisingly brilliant. Game Boy and Game Boy Color games fill nearly the entire panel. Vertical arcade shooters (shmups) run in their native portrait orientation by rotating the screen — a unique trick no other handheld here can do. PICO-8 games also shine on the 1:1 display.

🎵 The DAP Angle — This Matters for You

Anbernic explicitly marketed the RG Rotate as a hybrid gaming handheld + digital audio player (DAP). It ships with a cassette-themed music player, clock faces for desktop use, and — crucially — official USB-C DAC dongle support right on the product page. Since you already own a USB DAC dongle, this is a perfect match.

On Android, you can install USB Audio Player Pro (bypasses Android's internal audio mixer for bit-perfect playback), Square Pod (iPod-inspired UI), Spotify, Apple Music, or any streaming app. The Bluetooth 5.0 chip handles wireless headphones better than the BT 4.2 on the Brick. The device can even serve as a dedicated music source for your home speaker setup without draining your phone battery.

One reviewer noted: "The RG Rotate isn't the best retro handheld, and it definitely isn't the best music player. But there's just something about combining those two worlds."

☁️ Save Sync — The Easiest of the Three

Because the RG Rotate runs full Android 12, you can install Syncthing-Fork directly from F-Droid or the Play Store. No manual package installations, no CFW dependencies, no fragile terminal commands. The Android Syncthing experience is well-documented and actively maintained. You point it at your RetroArch saves folder and you're done.

For your use case — syncing saves across multiple handhelds including a home PC — Android is the path of least resistance. You can also sync saves from standalone emulators (PPSSPP, Drastic, DuckStation) via Syncthing, which is much harder to do consistently on Linux-based CFW.

Other Non-Gaming Perks

Beyond music playback, the RG Rotate offers clock face modes (four included), voice recording via the hidden microphone, full web browsing, and access to any Android app. The square screen in closed position gives it a desk-clock aesthetic that the other devices can't match. It's the only device here that can genuinely moonlight as a desktop companion.

📋 The Final Recommendation

🥇 Anbernic RG Rotate (Silver) — Best Fit for Your Needs

For someone with an existing Miyoo Mini, multiple handhelds, a USB DAC dongle, and an interest in non-gaming use, the RG Rotate is the most logical upgrade — and the most interesting one. It's the only device here that:

  • Officially supports USB-C DAC — your dongle will work, and UAPP can provide bit-perfect audio
  • Runs Android — Syncthing save sync is trivial to set up, and you can sync saves from Dolphin, PPSSPP, DuckStation, etc.
  • Doubles as a competent DAP — Square Pod + your DAC dongle = a charming offline music player that won't distract you with notifications
  • Feels genuinely different from your Miyoo Mini — the swivel is novel, the square screen changes how you think about retro games, and the aluminum build feels like a premium object

Caveats to accept: The 2,000 mAh battery is small — expect to charge daily. The 204 g weight (silver) is noticeable. And the square screen means 4:3 console games will be letterboxed. But for GB/GBC puzzles and RPGs? It's a match made in heaven.

Price: ~$108–$140 (Aurora Silver, aluminum body)

🥈 TrimUI Brick — Best Screen & Value

If the RG Rotate's quirks (square screen, weight, smaller battery) give you pause, the standard plastic TrimUI Brick is an excellent alternative. That 1024×768 screen is genuinely transformative for reading RPG text and seeing puzzle details. At ~$40–$60 it's the best value here. Save sync via Syncthing on CrossMix OS is possible but requires more effort than Android. USB DAC support is available on NextUI but not guaranteed on all CFW. The Brick Hammer is a luxury pick — only worth it if you truly value the metal feel.

Price: ~$40–$80 (plastic Brick), ~$90–$155 (Brick Hammer)

🥉 Miyoo Mini Plus — The Safe (But Incremental) Choice

The MM+ is undeniably great — Onion OS is a joy, the community support is unmatched, and it's the cheapest option. But for someone who already owns a Miyoo Mini, it's less of an upgrade and more of a size bump with Wi-Fi. If you find your original Mini too cramped and want to stay in the Onion ecosystem, it makes sense. Otherwise, the Brick and Rotate offer more meaningful differentiation.

Price: ~$50–$70

⚠️ Things You Might Not Have Thought Of

1. Save Sync Isn't "Set and Forget" on Any Device

Syncthing is powerful but requires discipline: always make an in-game save (not just a save state) before switching devices, close the emulator cleanly, and give the device a moment to sync before powering down. Save states are core/version-sensitive and can break across different CFW setups. The RG Rotate's Android foundation makes this easier but not effortless.

2. The RG Rotate "Screen Protected When Closed" Is a Real Advantage

The original Miyoo Mini is notorious for fragile screens — a single drop can shatter it. The Rotate's closed position protects the screen completely, which matters for a daily carry device. The Brick and MM+ both leave their screens exposed. Budget for a case if you go with either.

3. The Square Screen Trade-Off

The RG Rotate's 1:1 screen is brilliant for GB/GBC, PICO-8, vertical arcade games, and Neo Geo Pocket — but 4:3 console games (SNES, PS1, Genesis) will have black bars. If most of your RPGs are on SNES/PS1 rather than GBA/GB, the Brick's 4:3 screen may serve you better. For puzzle games, however, many are on GB/GBC/GBA/NGPC — all of which look great on the Rotate.

4. The RG Rotate Hinge — Early But Promising

The swivel hinge is the Rotate's biggest long-term question mark. One reviewer did a 1,000-cycle test and found only a hair's breadth of alignment shift afterward. Anbernic claims "high durability" alloy construction. It feels sturdy, but no one has had one for years. If hinge anxiety would bother you, the Brick is the safer mechanical bet.

5. Brick vs. Brick Hammer — The Value Equation

The Hammer costs roughly 2× what the plastic Brick costs for the same internals in a metal shell. For a daily carry device that will inevitably pick up scratches, the plastic Brick is the pragmatic choice. The Hammer is for collectors and those who prioritize hand-feel above all else. If you're going to spend Hammer money (~$100+), seriously consider whether the RG Rotate at a similar price offers more functional differentiation.
