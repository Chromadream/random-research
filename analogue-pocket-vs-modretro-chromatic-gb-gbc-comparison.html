🔨 Build Quality & Materials

Analogue Pocket

The Pocket uses a high-quality polycarbonate plastic shell that feels premium and modern. The front is protected by Gorilla Glass. At 315g it has noticeable heft — it feels substantial but some find it fatiguing for long sessions. The clean, minimalist aesthetic matches Analogue's design language.

Concern: The internal sealed Li-ion battery will degrade over years and is not user-replaceable without opening the device. If left fully drained for extended periods, the battery can be permanently damaged.

ModRetro Chromatic

The Chromatic takes build quality to another level with a magnesium alloy thixomolded shell — the same process used in high-end camera bodies and aerospace parts. It's cool to the touch, incredibly rigid, and ModRetro markets it as "indestructible" (their marketing shows it surviving being run over by a car). The display is protected by either Gorilla Glass or optional Sapphire Crystal (second only to diamond in hardness).

Longevity advantage: AA batteries mean no built-in battery to degrade. In 20 years, just pop in fresh AAs. The optional Rechargeable Power Core ($29.99 USD) is also user-replaceable.

🖥️ Screen Philosophy — Two Radically Different Approaches

This is perhaps the most important differentiator for GB/GBC purists, and one that casual buyers often overlook.

Pocket: High-Resolution Canvas

The 3.5" 1600×1440 LTPS LCD packs 615 ppi — exactly 10× the GBC's resolution in each dimension. This enables razor-sharp integer scaling with optional filters that can mimic original DMG, Pocket, GBC, and GBA subpixel grids. The larger screen is immersive and flexible.

However, because the panel is rotated 90° from its native orientation (to enable Tate mode for vertical shooters), a frame buffer is required, introducing approximately one frame of additional latency.

Chromatic: Native-Perfect Authenticity

The 2.56" IPS LCD runs at the exact native GBC resolution of 160×144 pixels — a custom panel built from scratch specifically for this device. No scaling, no filters, no interpretation. The colour gamut is subpixel-accurate to the original GBC palette. What you see is exactly what the original artists intended, just backlit and brighter.

The display is also specifically engineered for sunlight readability — reviewers confirm it's genuinely usable in direct midday sun. And because there's no rotation buffer, the Chromatic achieves sub-1-frame input latency, independently verified as faster than the Pocket.

🧠 Things Gamers Might Not Intuitively Consider

⚠️ Before you buy — the hidden factors

1. Battery Longevity & Heirloom Durability

The Analogue Pocket uses a sealed 4300 mAh Li-ion battery. Like all Li-ion cells, it will degrade over 3–5 years of regular use. If stored fully drained for months, it can enter a deep-discharge state and become permanently damaged. Replacement requires opening the device — not impossible, but not trivial either. In contrast, the Chromatic's AA battery design means zero internal battery degradation. The device should still work perfectly in 20 years with fresh batteries. This is a deliberate "heirloom" design choice that serious collectors should weigh carefully.

2. The Hidden Cost of Firmware Updates

The Analogue Pocket requires a microSD card (and a way to write to it) to update firmware. This is an extra purchase you might not think about. The Chromatic updates via USB-C cable to PC/Mac — no additional accessories needed.

3. D-Pad & Competitive Play

Multiple reviewers — including those who own both devices — consistently rate the Chromatic's D-pad as the best on any retro handheld. The PBT plastic, precise actuation, and lack of ghost inputs make a tangible difference in games like Tetris, Pokémon Puzzle Challenge, or precision platformers. Combined with the Chromatic's verified zero input latency advantage, competitive or speedrunning players have a clear reason to prefer it.

4. The IR Port Matters for Some Classics

The GBC's IR port wasn't just a gimmick. Games like Pokémon Trading Card Game (card transfer), Pokémon Gold/Silver/Crystal (Mystery Gift), and Mission: Impossible use it for actual gameplay features. The Pocket lacks IR entirely. The Chromatic includes a backwards-compatible IR emitter/receiver — a small detail that shows where ModRetro's priorities lie.

5. Weight & Actual Portability

At 175g vs 315g, the Chromatic is nearly half the weight of the Pocket. Combined with smaller dimensions, it is genuinely pocketable in a way the Pocket is not. For a device you plan to carry daily, this matters more than spec sheets suggest.

6. Save States — Convenience vs Authenticity

The Pocket offers save states and sleep/suspend, which are genuinely useful modern conveniences. The Chromatic deliberately omits them — ModRetro's philosophy is that working within each game's original save system is part of the authentic experience. This may frustrate some players (especially for games with punishing continue systems), but purists will appreciate the discipline it enforces. ModRetro has not ruled out adding save states via firmware in the future.

7. Flash Cart Compatibility

Both devices work with EverDrive and other flash carts, which is crucial for playing homebrew, fan translations, ROM hacks, and legally-obtained ROM backups without paying collector prices for rare cartridges. This levels the playing field for those who don't own extensive physical collections.

8. The Hidden WiFi/Bluetooth Hardware

Teardowns of the Chromatic reveal WiFi and Bluetooth chips on the mainboard that are currently unused. ModRetro has hinted at future features being enabled. This suggests the Chromatic may gain wireless capabilities down the line — something to watch.

9. Cartridge Updating (Cart Clinic)

ModRetro's Cart Clinic tool lets you update their published game cartridges directly through the Chromatic via USB-C. This means games like Tetris for Chromatic can receive patches, balance updates, and new features on the physical cartridge itself. This is genuinely new in the retro space and has no equivalent on the Pocket.

10. Ethical Considerations

ModRetro was founded by Palmer Luckey, who also runs Anduril Industries — a defence contractor producing autonomous military drones. This has been controversial in the retro gaming community. Whether this matters to you is a personal decision, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

11. Shipping & Import to Australia

Neither device has an Australian distributor. Both ship from the US. For the Pocket, Analogue charges ~$42–60 USD for shipping. Australian buyers also face 10% GST on the total value (goods + shipping). Some buyers use parcel forwarding services to reduce costs. ModRetro ships internationally with pricing listed in AUD on their storefront. Real-world reports suggest a Chromatic lands at approximately A$355–380 all-in, while a Pocket lands at approximately A$450–500 all-in once shipping and GST are factored in. Local Australian resellers often mark the Pocket up to A$600–800 on secondary markets.

🏆 Verdict: Which One Should YOU Buy for GB/GBC?

Buy the ModRetro Chromatic if…

  • You are a GB/GBC purist who wants the most authentic experience possible
  • Build quality, D-pad precision, and input latency are your top priorities
  • You value heirloom longevity — a device that will still work perfectly in 20 years
  • You want something truly pocketable and lightweight for daily carry
  • You want the lowest price and the most colour options
  • You appreciate the IR port and full GBC feature parity
  • You want a device that's actually in stock and ships immediately

Buy the Analogue Pocket if…

  • You value modern conveniences — save states, sleep mode, screenshots
  • You want the larger, sharper screen with filtering options
  • TV-out via Dock is important to you
  • You might want to branch out into GBA or other handheld libraries later
  • You want stereo speakers and a more modern OS interface
  • You're comfortable with the higher Australian landed cost and availability challenges
  • The Palmer Luckey / Anduril association is a dealbreaker for you

For the GB/GBC-only buyer in Australia, the ModRetro Chromatic is the stronger pure-play choice on build quality, authenticity, latency, portability, and value — if you can live without save states. The Analogue Pocket is the better choice if modern QoL features and future flexibility matter more to you than absolute authenticity.
