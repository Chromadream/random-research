Drop the optional slots and many more brands qualify. Every full-board brand above obviously works here too; these are the extra ones. Walrus covers all five with no gaps. Is a one-brand board worth it?

The real advantages are practical: matching power needs (Boss, Mooer and Donner pedals all run on standard 9V centre-negative), one warranty process, consistent knob layouts, and a board that looks deliberate.

The downside is that no brand is best at everything. Most players end up with a Boss tuner, someone else's drive and a third brand's reverb, and that's fine. Treat a single brand as a starting point you're free to break.

Which one, if you're doing it

  • Cheapest: Donner, NUX or Mooer for the whole board; Behringer if you only want the core four (about US$140).
  • Smallest: Mooer (every pedal is a micro) or a mostly-mini MXR or TC board.
  • Toughest: Boss compacts, which are famously hard to break.
  • Best power story: MXR (Iso-Brick) or Mooer (Micro Power), both isolated and made by the same brand.
  • Weirdest: Electro-Harmonix, whose catalogue also covers the oddball swaps.
