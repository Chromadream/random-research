Checkout list · after Room 215

Where to go after Hotel Dusk

Fourteen games picked for what makes Hotel Dusk work: one locked-in setting, a lot of talking to strangers with secrets, a melancholy lead, and puzzles that live inside the story. Each one says how long it runs and which of your handhelds to play it on.

Plot blurbs cover only the setup. Cover art and screenshots can't be embedded here (the page can only load images it ships with), so each card links out to a gallery instead.

Same studio: Cing

The closest you can get. Cing made Hotel Dusk and these four, and you can feel the same hands in the writing and the hardware-trick puzzles.

Last Window: The Secret of Cape West

DS · 2010 · EU/JP only
Length
12–15 h
Play on
DS Lite · PAL cart (DS is region-free)

A year on, Kyle Hyde is back in Los Angeles, out of work, and his apartment building is being sold out from under its tenants. A note arrives asking him to dig into the building's past, and the week before everyone moves out becomes a slow excavation of who lived there and why they never left.

It is the direct sequel: same rotoscoped sketch art, same book-style hold, same structure of chapter-end quizzes and conversations you can fail. It even ships with an in-game novel of the story.

Reviews split on whether it matches the original; the puzzles are thought to be weaker, the mood just as strong. It never came out in North America, so the English text is on the European release. Puzzles lean on the resistive screen, microphone and closing the lid, which is why real hardware wins.

Another Code: Recollection

Switch · 2024 · remake of DS 2005 + Wii 2009
Length
10–16 h for both games together
Play on
Switch

Ashley, about to turn fourteen, gets a letter from a father she believed was dead, inviting her to a remote island. The second story picks her up two years later at a lakeside campsite where her memories of her mother start surfacing.

Cing's other series: a quiet, closed setting, family secrets pried out through conversation and object puzzles. Lighter in tone than Kyle Hyde, but the same emotional register.

The remake simplifies puzzles and trims the Wii sequel heavily. For Cing's original hardware puzzles, play Trace Memory (DS, ~5 h) on the DS Lite instead.

Time Hollow

DS · 2008 · Konami
Length
4–8 h plus a short new-game-plus
Play on
DS Lite

Ethan wakes on his 17th birthday to find his parents vanished twelve years ago, a fact nobody else finds strange. He's holding a pen that can open small holes into the past, and fixing one thing tends to break another.

Not Cing, but directed by Junko Kawano of Shadow of Memories, and it plays like Hotel Dusk's younger cousin: anime cutscenes, a small town of suspects, and touch-driven puzzles that rewrite the story.

Short enough to finish in an evening or two. Puzzles involve a lot of stylus circling.

Glass Rose

PS2 · 2003 · Capcom, EU/JP
Length
~10–15 h est.
Play on
AYN Thor · NetherSX2  or  Ally X · PCSX2

A young reporter visits an abandoned mansion where a family was murdered decades ago, blacks out, and wakes in 1929 with the house restored and the family still alive. He can briefly read the thoughts of whoever he's talking to, and the killings haven't happened yet.

Cing's first game, written by Hotel Dusk writer Rika Suzuki. One building, a cast of guarded residents, a mystery tied to the past: the Hotel Dusk blueprint, four years earlier.

Rougher than the DS games, with a timed hour-by-hour structure that restarts you if you miss something. Use the English PAL release. There's no known mouse support, so the cursor moves on the analog stick, which reviewers found clunky.

Same shelf: DS and 3DS era

Different studios, same golden period of stylus-driven mysteries.

Jake Hunter Detective Story: Ghost of the Dusk

3DS · 2018 · Aksys
Length
~12–18 h est., across six cases
Play on
New 3DS XL · NA cart  or  AYN Thor · Azahar

A homeless man's death looks accidental, but it leads hard-boiled private eye Jake Hunter to a mansion whose owner insists the place is cursed. Five shorter side cases come bundled with the main one.

The longest-running hard-boiled detective adventure in Japan (as Jinguji Saburo), complete with smoky jazz, cigarettes as a thinking mechanic, and interrogation-heavy scenes. The closest tonal match to Kyle Hyde's world-weariness.

The 3DS eShop is closed, so it's physical only now. The localisation is serviceable rather than polished. Memories of the Past on DS is the older English entry if you want more; play that one on the DS Lite.

Ghost Trick: Phantom Detective

DS 2010 · Switch / PC remaster 2023
Length
~13 h
Play on
Switch  or  Ally X · Steam  or  DS Lite · original

A man dies in a junkyard with no memory of who he was. As a ghost who can possess objects and rewind the four minutes before a death, he has one night to find out who killed him.

Like Hotel Dusk, it all happens across a single night and it's built on rotoscoped-feeling, smooth character animation. Its plot clicks into place the way Hyde's case does.

More puzzle-game than visual novel; the puzzles are timing-based rather than conversational. The remaster adds redrawn art and an arranged soundtrack.

999: Nine Hours, Nine Persons

DS 2009 · PC/Switch in The Nonary Games
Length
~10 h to the first ending, more for the true one
Play on
Ally X · Nonary Games  or  DS Lite · original

Nine strangers wake up on a ship rigged to sink in nine hours, with numbered bracelets and a set of doors only certain combinations can open. Nobody knows why they were chosen.

A sealed location, a cast who each hide something, and a story that rewards keeping notes. It's darker than Hotel Dusk, but scratches the same piece-it-together itch.

Escape-room segments alternate with long reading stretches. Needs multiple playthroughs for the real ending; the Nonary Games flowchart makes that painless.

Shadow of Memories

PS2 2001 · also PC, PSP
Length
~5–7 h per run, with many endings
Play on
AYANEO 2 · PCSX2  or  Pocket S2 · PPSSPP (PSP port)

Eike is stabbed in a small German town and wakes in a café with a strange device and a chance to prevent his own death. He has a day to work out who wants him dead, hopping back through the town's history to change it.

The earlier game from Time Hollow's director. It's slow, talky, and atmospheric, and it trusts you to piece a mystery together from conversations across eras.

Modern heirs

Newer games that carry the same mood, mostly on Switch or Steam.

Famicom Detective Club: The Missing Heir & The Girl Who Stands Behind

Switch · 2021 remakes of 1988–89 originals
Length
~8 h + ~10 h
Play on
Switch · in release order

A young assistant detective with amnesia investigates the death of a wealthy matriarch in a superstitious village; in the second game, a schoolgirl's murder ties back to a ghost story told at her school.

Nintendo's own Japanese detective adventures, remade with animated portraits and full voice acting. Methodical questioning, an old-fashioned menu verb system, and creeping rural dread.

Emio – The Smiling Man: Famicom Detective Club

Switch · 2024
Length
~11 h
Play on
Switch · after the two remakes

A student is found dead with a paper bag over his head, drawn with a smile. It matches an urban legend about a killer from years earlier, and the agency reopens a case the police never closed.

An entirely new story in the series, more mature in tone, and its ending sparked as much discussion as Hotel Dusk's did.

Paranormasight: The Seven Mysteries of Honjo

Switch / PC · 2023 · Square Enix
Length
~12 h
Play on
Ally X · Steam  or  Switch

In 1980s Tokyo, several people are drawn into a curse ritual tied to old local legends, where anyone who collects enough souls can bring someone back. You switch between their stories as they cross paths over one night.

An 80s setting, a flowchart of interlocking characters, and a quietly sad heart under the horror. It also breaks the fourth wall in ways Cing would have enjoyed.

Root Letter

Vita 2016 · PC/Switch Last Answer 2018
Length
~10 h for one ending
Play on
Ally X · Steam (Last Answer)

Fifteen years after his high-school pen pal stopped writing, a man rereads her last letter, which seems to confess to a crime. He travels to her town in Shimane to find out who she really was.

An adult man piecing together a stranger's past through interviews, letters, and a place that has moved on. It's the closest structural echo of Hyde's search for Bradley.

The ending is divisive and the confrontation scenes are a bit hammy.

Kentucky Route Zero

PC / Switch · 2013–2020
Length
~10 h across five acts
Play on
Ally X · Steam  or  Switch

An aging antiques delivery driver needs to make one last drop at an address no one can find, somewhere along a hidden highway running beneath the Kentucky caves.

No puzzles to speak of, but if what stayed with you was Hotel Dusk's lonely late-night mood and its delivery-man lead, this is that feeling stretched across a whole game.

Disco Elysium: The Final Cut

PC / Switch / consoles · 2019–21
Length
~25 h
Play on
Ally X · Steam, docked

A detective wakes up in a wrecked hotel room with no memory of who he is, and a body is hanging behind the hostel outside. He has to solve it with a partner he doesn't remember meeting.

The modern peak of "conversation is the gameplay." It's a broken ex-cop, a hotel, and a murder, with interrogations you can fumble.

Twice the length of anything else here and far more political and wordy, so a big screen helps.
