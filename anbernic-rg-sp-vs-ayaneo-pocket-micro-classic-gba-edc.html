Anbernic RG SP vs. Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic

GBA Focus EDC / Everyday Carry  — The definitive head-to-head for the pure 2D-era retro enthusiast who already owns a Pocket Micro Classic

🔍 At a Glance

The Anbernic RG SP (launched August 5, 2026) is Anbernic's third and most refined GBA SP-inspired clamshell. It drops analog sticks entirely and focuses on a slim, pocketable, $65 Linux-powered 2D powerhouse. The Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic (launched March 2025) is a premium Android candybar inspired by the Game Boy Micro, with an aluminum/glass build starting at $179.

Both devices are stickless, D-pad-only handhelds with 3:2 IPS displays purpose-built for integer-scaled GBA emulation — but they approach the "EDC GBA device" mission from dramatically different angles.

⚖️ Pros & Cons for Each Device

🟠 Anbernic RG SP — Pros

  • Incredible value at ~$65
  • Clamshell = screen self-protects, no case needed for EDC
  • Lighter (168g) & more compact when closed
  • Hall sensor: open-to-wake, close-to-sleep — pure pickup-and-play
  • Better battery life (3300mAh + efficient Linux)
  • HDMI out for TV play
  • 3.5mm headphone jack
  • Strong custom firmware ecosystem (muOS, KNULLI)
  • Dual microSD slots
  • Perfect 3× GBA integer scaling with no black bars

🟠 Anbernic RG SP — Cons

  • Plastic build — not premium
  • Only 1GB RAM; weak for shaders
  • Mono speaker
  • H700 can't handle N64/Dreamcast well (moot for GBA-only use)
  • Lower PPI (254 vs 330)
  • Device can warm up with Wi-Fi on
  • Not yet released — firmware support still maturing

🟢 Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic — Pros

  • Stunning 4× GBA integer scale at 330 PPI — true endgame GBA screen
  • Premium aluminum/glass build, gorgeous Famicom-style colors
  • Massive headroom: Helio G99 runs heavy CRT shaders effortlessly
  • 6–8 GB RAM means flawless multi-tasking, Android games, streaming
  • Active cooling — sustained performance without throttling
  • Fingerprint sensor for quick unlock
  • Stereo speakers (though muffled)
  • Bluetooth 5.2
  • Android = cloud streaming (GeForce Now, Xbox), native Android games

🟢 Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic — Cons

  • Expensive — 3× the price of the RG SP
  • Heavy (227g) for its size
  • Exposed glass front — needs a case/sleeve for EDC
  • No 3.5mm headphone jack
  • No TV-out
  • Weaker battery (2600mAh) with Android overhead
  • Start/Select button placement is awkward — accidental home-screen exits
  • Android setup is more involved than Linux pick-up-and-play
  • Fan reportedly very quiet/possibly not engaging correctly (reviewer reports)

🏆 Final Verdict

For pure GBA EDC: The Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic is the better device — its 4× integer-scaled screen at 330 PPI is genuinely the best GBA display on any retro handheld. The premium build, shader capability, and Android flexibility make it the "endgame GBA device" as multiple reviewers have called it.

But the Anbernic RG SP is the better EDC device. The clamshell design means you can literally toss it in a pocket with zero concern for the screen. The Hall-sensor open-to-play is faster than any Android device. It's 35% lighter. It costs less than a nice dinner. If you already own the PMC, the RG SP makes a compelling companion — the one you grab when you're heading out the door and don't want to baby a $200+ piece of aluminum and glass.

Bottom line: They're not competitors — they're complementary. The PMC is your at-home/show-off GBA machine. The RG SP is your actual EDC beater. At $65, owning both is not unreasonable.

🔔 Things You Didn't Ask (But Should Know)

  • ⚠️ RG SP RAM Roulette: Anbernic has a history of quietly downgrading RAM on its H700 devices mid-production. The RG34XXSP launched with 2GB, was downgraded to 1GB, and some teardowns found 512MB. The RG SP currently ships with 1GB, but this could change. For GBA-only, even 512MB is fine — but it's something to watch.
  • ⚠️ PMC Fan Concerns: Multiple reviewers reported that the Pocket Micro Classic's active cooling fan either doesn't spin audibly or may not engage correctly. Ayaneo claims it activates above 40°C. This hasn't caused overheating issues in practice, but hardware QC on the fan is a known question mark.
  • 🔌 USB-C Cable Compatibility: The RG SP is reported to have some pickiness with USB-C cables. Some C-to-C cables may not charge it properly; A-to-C is safer.
  • 🎧 No Headphone Jack on PMC: The Ayaneo Pocket Micro Classic has no 3.5mm jack. If you use wired headphones, you'll need a USB-C DAC/adapter. The RG SP has a standard headphone jack.
  • 🖥️ PMC Has No Video Out: You cannot connect the Pocket Micro Classic to a TV or external monitor at all. The RG SP has mini HDMI out and supports Bluetooth controllers for couch play.
  • 📦 RG SP Launch Discount: Anbernic offered a $7 early-bird discount for the first 3 days, bringing the base model to $57.99. The standard price is $64.99, but Anbernic frequently runs sales.
  • 🔄 The KONKR Pocket Advance: Ayaneo is also releasing a GBA (original horizontal) inspired device called the KONKR Pocket Advance. If the clamshell vs. Micro debate doesn't settle it, there's a third Ayaneo GBA homage on the horizon. Details are still scarce as of mid-2026.
