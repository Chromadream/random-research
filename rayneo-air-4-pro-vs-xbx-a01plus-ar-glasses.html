RayNeo Air 4 Pro vs X by Xreal a01+ (XBX a01+)

A head-to-head comparison of two $299 AR/XR "wearable display" glasses, with a primary focus on movie watching — plus notes on casual gaming and productivity trials. All facts sourced from independent reviews and official product pages (links at the bottom).

⚡ Bottom line up front

For a movie-watching-first buyer, the RayNeo Air 4 Pro is generally the stronger pick: it projects a much larger virtual image (a ~201-inch screen vs. 147 inches), has noticeably better Bang & Olufsen-tuned quad speakers, more saturated/accurate color, and was the first to bring HDR10 to the category.

The X by Xreal a01+ counters with a much brighter panel (1,600 vs 1,200 nits), a wider 50° field of view, and a dramatically lighter, more comfortable 62 g frame (vs 76 g) — it is the better "wear it for a whole flight and forget it's there" device. Reviewers consistently say the a01+ wins on comfort/design/brightness, while the Air 4 Pro wins on audio, color vividness, and raw sharpness.

Short version: pick the Air 4 Pro for the best home-theater-like picture & sound; pick the a01+ for the lightest, brightest, most comfortable travel screen. Neither has true spatial anchoring — both are effectively fixed "screen-on-your-face" displays at this price.

2. The main event: movie watching 🎬

Both glasses are USB-C "external monitors on your face" — they have no battery, no OS, and just mirror whatever you plug in. For film, the differences come down to screen size, brightness, color, and audio.

RayNeo Air 4 Pro — the better cinema

  • Bigger picture: a ~201-inch virtual image (vs 147") gives a more genuinely theatrical scale. Android Police found widescreen 16:9 movies "truly immersive" and "easy to forget the world around you."
  • Better sound: the 4-driver Bang & Olufsen-tuned system gets clear, detailed dialog and a real surround effect. Gizmodo and Tom's Guide both found it clearly more nuanced than the a01+'s stereo speakers (which are described as "tinny," lacking low/mid punch, and not as loud).
  • Richer color: 98% DCI-P3 with ΔE<2 factory accuracy; Tom's Guide says RayNeo is "more colorful out of the box" and shows better "raw sharpness."
  • HDR10 first-mover: real HDR10 playback plus an AI SDR→HDR mode that works well (Tom's Hardware called the visual delta vs SDR "minimal but a tad richer"; Tom's Guide called HDR10 support "huge").

X by Xreal a01+ — the brighter, lighter travel screen

  • 1,600 nits peak brightness vs 1,200 — noticeably better in bright rooms/daylight (Engadget: "sharp image in direct sun"). Helpful if you can't black out your environment.
  • 50° FOV vs 47° — slightly wider, with reviewers noting less edge blur than the Air 4 Pro (Tom's Guide).
  • 62 g and a smaller, harder case — the clear winner for long-haul comfort (Android Headlines wore it for a 7-hour flight without issue).
  • Its "Cinema" color mode adds a warm, filmic tone; the AI SDR→HDR converter adds "pop of color" with minimal latency.

Watch-outs for both

  • Edge blur: both suffer some blur/chromatic aberration at the edges (worse on the Air 4 Pro per Tom's Guide; noted on both by multiple outlets). Keep the virtual screen at "medium" if your device lets you size it.
  • HDR10 quirks: Android Police saw distracting flicker in HDR10 mode on the Air 4 Pro (not in SDR/AI-HDR). On the a01+, engaging HDR modes drops refresh to 60/90 Hz. Real HDR also often requires premium streaming tiers (Netflix Ultra, Disney+ Premium).
  • Light bleed: neither has electrochromic dimming. Use the included snap-on shade/light shield for the best blacks; the Air 4 Pro's shade is easy to knock off (Android Police).
  • Audio leakage: both leak sound to people nearby. RayNeo offers a "Whisper" mode and optional SoundTube ($15) for privacy; the a01+ has a "Private" mode but reviewers noticed little difference between its sound modes.

5. Gaming 🎮 (minimal use)

  • Both run at up to 120 Hz and are plug-and-play with Steam Deck, ROG Ally, and other DP-alt-mode handhelds. Both are genuinely fun for handheld gaming on a huge virtual screen.
  • The a01+ has a slight edge for bright, high-refresh action in SDR (120 Hz + 1,600 nits), but remember its HDR modes cap refresh at 60/90 Hz. One reviewer (Android Headlines) went further and said the a01+ isn't a gaming pick at all, recommending Xreal's 240 Hz ROG R1 for serious play.
  • The Air 4 Pro adds a dedicated "Game" display mode and, crucially, works with Nintendo Switch/Switch 2 via the official RayNeo JoyDock ($89) or HDMI adapter. The a01+ does not support the Switch natively — you'd need a third-party dock.
  • Neither has motion-to-photon tricks like 3DoF anchoring; for fast-moving shooters the lack of anchoring is fine since the screen follows your head.

6. Productivity 💼 (trials only)

  • RayNeo is the better of the two for text: Tom's Guide needed to magnify UI to read small text on the a01+ but not on the Air 4 Pro; its headline quip for the a01+ was "just don't try to read an email on them." RayNeo also offers a Windows "Mirror Studio" app for multiple virtual monitors.
  • Reality check for both: 1080p-per-eye at ~47–50° FOV is limiting. Multiple outlets note fixed/no IPD adjustment and edge blur make dense spreadsheets, coding, and multi-window work frustrating. Engadget (on the a01+) said 1080p "makes it tough to multitask," and Android Authority (on the Air 4 Pro) reported at least one edge is "always a bit fuzzy."
  • If productivity matters more than movies, reviewers point to pricier 3DoF options (Xreal One/1S, Viture) — but for occasional email/notes on a plane, both are usable.

7. Things reviewers flagged that you'll want to plan for

  • No battery = heavy drain on your phone. Measured examples: Air 4 Pro pulled ~20% in under an hour on a Pixel 9 Pro (Android Authority) and ~13–15% per ~45 min (Gizmodo on a01+, iPhone 17). For long-haul flights, budget for a charging adapter: RayNeo sells a $35 USB-C charging adapter; Xreal sells the "XREAL Hub."
  • DP Alt Mode is mandatory. Both need a USB-C port that actually outputs DisplayPort video. Some phones (e.g., certain OnePlus/Nothing/Motorola models) won't work with the Air 4 Pro. iPhone 15/16/17 and most iPads/Macs/PCs are fine.
  • Prescription wearers must buy inserts. Neither is comfortable over glasses. RayNeo partners with Lensology; the a01+ inserts from HonsVR run ~$50. Factor this into the real cost.
  • Measure your IPD. Both have fixed optics. The a01+ officially covers 54.5–74.5 mm; the Air 4 Pro has no published adjustable IPD and no image shifting. If you're far outside the sweet spot, expect blur.
  • 3D support differs. The Air 4 Pro can do native SBS 3D and AI 2D→3D via its app (Gizmodo notes this as an advantage). The a01+ supports SBS 3D but cannot convert 2D→3D, and it has no companion app.
  • Sound privacy: both leak audio. RayNeo's Whisper mode + SoundTube accessory help; the a01+'s "Private" mode helps less than advertised.
  • No cameras or AI: both are pure display glasses — a privacy plus some reviewers specifically called out.
