Mangmi Air Y Pro (8GB) vs Anbernic RG477V

Baseline: AYANEO Pocket DMG (no true right stick). Use case: PS2 / original Xbox / GameCube / Wii, plus Xbox 360 if Xendroid+ is good enough. Researched November 2026.

Verdict: if you buy one, buy the RG477V. It is roughly on par with the Pocket DMG for PS2/GC (up to ~2.25x), adds a real right stick, has a 4.7" 4:3 120Hz panel with BFI, and gets ~5h of PS2/GC play. The catch is its Mali GPU: weaker for Dolphin, original Xbox and PC emulation. The Air Y Pro 8GB is ~US$70 cheaper, on the well-supported Snapdragon 865 + Turnip path, but tops out around 2x, its panel has documented ghosting, the L2/R2 rocker is disliked, and battery is ~3h. Neither is an Xbox 360 device; Xendroid+ is legitimate but experimental and targets Adreno 7xx/8xx.

Australian pricing

  • RG477V: Anbernic AU storefront US$259.99 (8/128). Amazon AU third-party ~AU$470–477; eBay AU ~AU$422–479; Anbernic official eBay from ~AU$524. Price has risen since launch, with resellers citing global memory supply constraints.
  • Air Y Pro: no Australian retailer found. Direct from mangmi.com at US$189.99 ≈ AU$265 before shipping/GST. Used units seen on eBay AU at ~AU$229.
  • Pocket DMG (reference): from AU$629.95 at The Gamesmen.

Spec takeaways

  • CPU (the PS2 bottleneck): the RG477V's A715s are the strongest on paper; in Android Authority's Geekbench 6 testing it beat a Snapdragon 865 stand-in. The Air Y Pro's A77s are a 2020 flagship design.
  • GPU: the DMG's Adreno 740-class part is clearly ahead, and it is the class the 360 projects target.
  • RAM: 8GB is the minimum on the Air Y Pro; reviewers found the 4GB model at the edge in ARMSX2.

2. Controls: the right-stick question

Both fix the DMG's main problem with a real second Hall-effect stick (no TMR on either). The DMG's touchpad is fine for camera control but poor for aiming; DROIX called it not an effective substitute for analog controls.

Mangmi Air Y Pro

  • Dual Hall sticks, low and symmetrical — serviceable, though Retro Handhelds calls placement awkward.
  • Digital L2/R2 on a single rear rocker (same idea as the DMG): pressing one lifts the other. Widely called the cheapest-feeling part.
  • Large, loose D-pad with lots of pivot; diagonals can misfire.
  • Quiet face buttons with good travel. Volume wheel, programmable button, 6-axis gyro.
  • Soft-touch shell smudges; slightly top-heavy.

Anbernic RG477V

  • Dual Hall sticks with RGB rings, inset near the centre; right thumb doesn't rub the stick on face buttons.
  • Large square deadzones out of the box — tune in-emulator.
  • Digital microswitch triggers placed too far out; L1/R1 small. Hurts twin-stick shooters and racers.
  • D-pad a bit soft but accurate.
  • Glass front collects fingerprints. Comfort opinions split (most fine; Joey found it top-heavy).

Bottom line: neither has analog triggers, so racing games lose nothing vs the DMG. The RG477V's right stick is better placed but its shoulders are worse; the Air Y Pro's shoulders are better placed but feel worse.

3. Emulation vs the Pocket DMG

Baseline: the DMG handles GC/PS2 at 3x comfortably, with Adreno/Turnip and the GPU class every Xbox/360 project targets.

PS2 (NetherSX2 / ARMSX2)

  • RG477V: Android Authority saw no slowdown in tested PS2/GC games on the lowest “Auto” profile at about native panel res; Retro Game Corps rates up to 2.25x. The Dimensity 8300 is one of two chips ARMSX2 optimises against: v2.6.9 cut Xenosaga frame times 84% on it, and 22/22 project test games now fit the worst-case frame budget (up from 16). Vulkan auto-selected.
  • MaliSX2: ARMSX2 2.8.2 shipped a custom Mali driver for G615/G715, broadened as MaliSX2 in 2.8.3. The headline Ace Combat result was on a Mali-G57, not the G615.
  • RG477V quirk: use “Performance” or “Auto”, not “High Performance” (throttles sooner; 105°C on pre-release firmware).
  • Air Y Pro: 2x fine with the usual problem games; demanding titles need per-game settings. ARMSX2 2.6.9 cut Xenosaga frame times 38% on the 865.
  • vs DMG: RG477V ≈ DMG; Air Y Pro a tier below (2x vs 3x; CPU-heavy titles like Xenosaga/Burnout/MGS3 hit the A77 limit first).

GameCube / Wii (Dolphin)

  • Air Y Pro: GameCube near-perfect; ~2x in most, 1–1.5x in heavy games. Adreno 650 + Turnip is the mature Dolphin path.
  • RG477V: fine on the stock Mali driver up to ~2.25x; heavier titles need tweaks. Mali's weak spots are driver-specific graphical bugs and shader stutter rather than raw speed.
  • MaliSX2 in Dolphin: dev builds only; Android Authority saw no significant boost on the RG477V in Nov 2026.
  • Wii pointer: both have gyro and a right stick — far more practical than the DMG's touchpad.

Original Xbox (hakuX / X1 BOX)

  • hakuX 1.0.0 (23 Oct 2026) replaced xemu's CPU path with FEX x86→ARM translation and a native kernel reimplementation (no BIOS). 1.1.0 added experimental Mali/Xclipse/PowerVR support. Rated on 8 Gen 2 devices (Retroid Pocket Nova, AYN Thor) with Turnip T30.
  • X1 BOX is a contested xemu port (publicly criticised by the xemu developer); it now defaults to the hakuX core. Upstream xemu still has no official Android build.
  • Air Y Pro: gets the main Turnip path, with far less GPU headroom. Lighter games (Halo, DOA3) reported playable on SD865 hardware.
  • RG477V: experimental Mali path (a HakuX-Mali fork tested on the D8300 RG477M). Expect it to work with more glitches.
  • DMG: closest of the three to the hakuX reference hardware.

Driver summary

Adreno + Turnip (Air Y Pro, DMG, Thor) is still the safe bet for Dolphin, hakuX, Xendroid+ and PC (Winlator/GameHub). Mali (RG477V) is now very good for PS2 thanks to direct D8300 tuning but second-class elsewhere: PC emulation barely functional, though Switch via Eden was playable for a healthy number of games.

4. Xendroid+ (Xbox 360)

  • What / who: Android Xbox 360 emulator by GitHub user phforner0, forked from rfandango's XenDroid (itself rebased onto Xenia Edge). Xenia/Canary code, ARM64 JIT, Vulkan, tuned for Snapdragon/Adreno.
  • Legitimacy: open source (AGPL-3.0 since build 87, 18 Oct 2026), free, non-commercial. Stable v0.2.2 (4 Nov 2026), previews ongoing (v152, 9 Nov). No scam or vibe-coding accusations found — unlike X1 BOX, “PSX2” and the empty “Xanite” app.
  • Download only from github.com/phforner0/Xendroid-Plus or phforner0.github.io. The SEO-style xendroid.org looks unofficial. Upstream warns that anyone who paid for it was scammed.
  • Requirements: Android 10+, arm64, Vulkan; Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 or newer recommended. Custom drivers load only on Adreno.

5. Known issues & reception

Mangmi Air Y Pro

  • Ghosting: same 4.2" panel as the AYANEO Pocket Air Mini / Pocket S Mini — you can judge it on yours. Joey called it extreme; Retro Dodo very minor after an update; Zelpio says later firmware reduces it significantly.
  • Slightly-under-60Hz refresh; buggy pre-launch launcher; locked Android/data (breaks Syncthing workflows); no per-app profiles; weak speakers.
  • Reception: Retro Dodo 7.3; Held Games 4.1/5 as a value play, flagging Mangmi's short support history; Joey says the RG477V wins on most metrics for ~$60 more.

Anbernic RG477V

  • Early firmware ran hot in High Performance (fixed by OTA). A community unbricker exists, suggesting some failed updates.
  • V1.38 (May 2026): RG Home frontend, Control Center, key mapping. V1.39: fixed sticks freezing when LEDs toggled, SystemUI crashes, intermittent trigger dropouts. Offline packages for failed OTAs.
  • Reception: Retro Game Corps called it probably Anbernic's best vertical; Android Authority Editor's Choice (cons: cramped triggers, Mali drivers).

Pocket DMG context: touchpad as right-stick substitute, some portrait-panel jelly scrolling, high price, fan up to 67dB in max mode.

Caveats

  • Most device performance claims come from launch reviews (RG477V Dec 2025–Jan 2026; Air Y Pro Aug–Sep 2026). Emulator updates since then (ARMSX2 2.6.9/2.8.x, hakuX 1.x, MaliSX2) have mostly favoured the RG477V.
  • Spec sheets conflict on RG477V RAM type, storage and battery; most sources agree on LPDDR5 / UFS / 5,500mAh. Some call its panel IPS; it's LTPS in-cell LCD.
  • All Xendroid+ fps figures are the developer's, on a Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 phone; no independent Xendroid+ tests were found on SD865, D8300 or G3x Gen 2. Reddit wasn't accessible.
  • AUD conversions are approximate and exclude shipping, GST and fees.
